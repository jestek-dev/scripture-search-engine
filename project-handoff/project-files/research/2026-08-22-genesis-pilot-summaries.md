# Genesis — book summary (draft)

## Short

Genesis is the Bible's book of beginnings. Its opening chapters tell how God made a good world and gave human beings, made in his image, charge of it — and how they doubted his word and his goodness, ate from the one tree he had forbidden, and set loose an unraveling that runs from Eden to the flood to Babel. Then the story narrows to one family: God calls Abraham and promises that through his offspring every family of earth will be blessed. Four flawed generations — Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Jacob's twelve sons — carry that promise down to Egypt, where the book closes on Joseph's verdict over it all: what people meant for evil, God meant for good.

## Full

Genesis opens the Bible, and everything that follows grows out of it. Its name means "origin," and it reads as a chain of family records — "this is the history of the generations of..." — that begins with the whole universe and ends beside a coffin in Egypt.

The book moves in two great arcs. Chapters 1–11 span the whole world. God creates an ordered, good world out of darkness and emptiness, makes man and woman in his own image, blesses them, and gives them the earth to tend and govern. But the man and the woman trust the serpent's word over God's and eat from the one tree God forbade, and the damage spreads outward: shame and blame in the garden, a brother's murder, Lamech's escalating violence, a world so corrupt that God grieves and cleanses it with the flood, and finally Babel, where a united humanity builds a tower to win itself a great name. Even amid judgment, grace keeps surfacing — a promise that the woman's offspring will bruise the serpent's head, a ship of rescue (our WEB translation's word for the familiar "ark"), a rainbow covenant.

Chapters 12–50 narrow to one family. Out of the scattered nations God calls Abraham, promising him land, offspring, and a great name — and, through him, blessing for all the families of the earth. The remainder of the book traces that promise through four generations: Abraham and Sarah, who wait decades for Isaac and stumble along the way; Isaac and Rebekah; Jacob, the schemer who wrestles with God and is renamed Israel; and Jacob's twelve sons, whose jealousy sells Joseph into Egypt. In every generation the pattern repeats: the family's own failures put the promise at risk, and God's faithfulness carries it through anyway. Covenant, blessing, and the long wait for a promised child are the book's recurring notes.

Genesis states its own message near the end. Standing before the brothers who sold him, Joseph says: you meant evil against me, but God meant it for good, to save many people alive (50:20). The book closes deliberately unfinished — Abraham's descendants are numerous but landless, living in Egypt, with Joseph's bones waiting under oath to be carried home. The promises are still alive, and the story leans forward into Exodus.

# The fifty chapters

## Genesis 1
**Summary:** God creates the heavens and the earth, speaking order and life into darkness and emptiness across six days — light, sky, seas and land, plants, sun and moon and stars, sea creatures and birds, land animals. Last of all he makes man and woman in his own image, blesses them, and appoints them to fill the earth and rule it. God surveys all he has made and calls it very good.
**Tags:** `creation` — the six-day creation account; existing anchor at "In the beginning, God created" (1:1); `image-of-god` — "God created man in his own image. In God's image he created him; male and female he created them" (1:26–27)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing

## Genesis 2
**Summary:** The creation week closes with God resting on the seventh day, blessing it and making it holy. The story then draws close: God forms the man from dust, plants a garden in Eden for him to cultivate and keep, and gives one command concerning the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. Since it is not good for the man to be alone, God makes the woman from one of the man's ribs, and the two become the pattern for marriage — one flesh, naked and unashamed.
**Tags:** `sabbath-rest` — God rests on the seventh day, blesses it, and makes it holy (2:2–3); `work-and-diligence` — man is placed in the garden "to cultivate and keep it" (2:15; existing anchor); `pastoral-marriage-divorce-teaching` — the verse Jesus cites on marriage: leave, join, one flesh (2:24; existing anchor); `godly-marriage` — the first marriage: a helper comparable to him, brought to the man (2:18–24); `creation` — the making of man and woman retold from the ground up (2:4–7, 21–22); `the-breath-of-life` — the concept's namesake text: "The LORD God formed man from the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul." (2:7, fixture-verified; honest note carried from the concept map: "living soul" here names the whole living person, so this verse teaches God-breathed life rather than a separable spirit)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing
**Sections:**
- **2:1–3 — The Seventh Day** (title: BSB) — God finishes the work of creation and rests on the seventh day, blessing it and setting it apart as holy.
- **2:4–25 — Man and Woman in the Garden** (title: BSB) — God forms the man, settles him in Eden to work it, gives the command about the forbidden tree, and makes the woman as his matching companion — the first marriage.

## Genesis 3
**Summary:** The serpent questions God's word, and the woman and the man eat the forbidden fruit, grasping at a wisdom God had withheld. Shame, hiding, and blame follow. God pronounces consequences — pain, a cursed ground, and a return to dust — yet the sentence on the serpent carries a promise: the woman's offspring will bruise the serpent's head (3:15). God clothes the couple in skins and sends them out of Eden, barring the way back to the tree of life.
**Tags:** `sin` — the first sin and its unfolding consequences, from shame to death (3:6–19); `divine-judgment` — judgment pronounced on serpent, woman, and man — curses falling on the serpent and the ground (3:14–19) — and the expulsion from Eden (3:22–24); `temptation` — Scripture's first temptation narrative: "Has God really said...?", and the tree seen as "good for food... a delight to the eyes... to be desired to make one wise" (3:1–6); `mortality` — the sentence of death enters the human story: "For you are dust, and you shall return to dust" (3:19), and the way back to the tree of life is barred (3:22–24)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing

## Genesis 4
**Summary:** Adam and Eve's first sons bring offerings to the LORD: Abel's is accepted, Cain's is not. Warned that sin is crouching at his door, Cain gives in anyway and murders his brother, then is sent out to wander east of Eden under a protective mark. Cain builds the first city, his descendants father its crafts — herds, music, metalwork — and violence swells in Lamech's boast of seventy-sevenfold revenge. The chapter closes on a different note: Seth is born, and people begin to call on the LORD's name.
**Tags:** `sin` — "sin crouches at the door; its desire is for you, but you are to rule over it" (4:7), then the first murder and its curse (4:8–12); `worship` — the first offerings brought to the LORD, and men beginning to call on the LORD's name (4:3–4, 26); `envy-and-jealousy` — Cain's burning anger when the LORD respects Abel's offering and not his (4:4–5); `temptation` — the Bible's first warning against temptation's pull: "sin crouches at the door; its desire is for you, but you are to rule over it" — warned, Cain gives in anyway (4:7–8); `vengeance` — Lamech's boast of seventy-sevenfold revenge for a wound, escalating vengeance narrated, never commended (4:23–24)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Genesis 5
**Summary:** A family record runs ten names from Adam to Noah. It opens by recalling that God made mankind in his likeness, then settles into a refrain — each man lives long, fathers children, "then he died" — the steady toll of mortality after Eden. Enoch breaks the pattern: he walked with God, and God took him. The list ends with Noah and his three sons, on the doorstep of the flood.
**Tags:** `presence-of-god` — Enoch "walked with God, and he was not found, for God took him" (5:22–24); `creation` — the record restates that God created man in his likeness, male and female, and blessed them (5:1–2); `mortality` — the chapter's refrain, eight times over: "then he died" (5:5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 27, 31) — the steady toll of mortality after Eden
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Genesis 6
**Summary:** As humanity multiplies, so does its corruption: the "sons of God" take wives as they please, the Nephilim appear, and the LORD sees that every thought of the human heart is only evil. Grieved at heart, God resolves to wipe out what he has made — but Noah finds favor in his eyes. God tells righteous Noah that a flood is coming, gives him the ship's dimensions, and promises to establish his covenant with him; Noah does all that God commands.
**Tags:** `sin` — wickedness great on the earth, every imagination of the heart only evil, the earth filled with violence (6:5, 11–12); `obedience-to-the-word` — "Thus Noah did. He did all that God commanded him" (6:22)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing
**Sections:**
- **6:1–7 — Corruption on the Earth** (title: BSB) — The sons of God, the Nephilim, and hearts bent wholly toward evil fill the earth, until the grieved LORD resolves to destroy what he has made.
- **6:8–22 — Noah's Favor and the Ark** (title: adapted from BSB) — Noah finds favor in the LORD's eyes. God tells the blameless man that he will end all flesh, gives him detailed instructions for the ship, promises a covenant, and Noah obeys completely.

## Genesis 7
**Summary:** At God's command Noah, his family, and pairs of every living creature enter the ship, and the LORD shuts them in. The fountains of the deep burst open, rain falls forty days and nights, and the waters climb until the highest mountains are covered. Every breathing creature outside the ship dies; only Noah and those with him are left, and the flood stands on the earth a hundred and fifty days.
**Tags:** `gods-protection` — "the LORD shut him in" (7:16); only Noah and those with him in the ship are preserved (7:23); `obedience-to-the-word` — "Noah did everything that the LORD commanded him" (7:5)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Genesis 8
**Summary:** God remembers Noah, and the waters begin their long retreat until the ship rests on the mountains of Ararat. Noah tests the ground with a raven and then a dove, whose olive leaf signals a drying world, and at God's word everyone comes out. Noah's first act is to build an altar and offer sacrifices, and the LORD commits himself to the world's future: never again to strike every living thing; while the earth remains, seedtime and harvest will not cease.
**Tags:** `gods-faithfulness` — God remembers Noah (8:1) and pledges that the earth's rhythms will not cease (8:21–22); `worship` — the first act on dry ground is an altar and burnt offerings to the LORD (8:20)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Genesis 9
**Summary:** God blesses Noah's family as a fresh start for humanity — be fruitful, fill the earth — permitting meat for food but forbidding blood, and demanding an accounting for human life because mankind bears God's image. He then makes a covenant with every living creature never to flood the earth again, setting the rainbow as its sign. Yet the new beginning is soon stained: Noah gets drunk and lies exposed, Ham dishonors him, and Noah's waking words curse Canaan, with blessing tied to Shem and Japheth.
**Tags:** `covenant` — the Bible's first explicit covenant enactment: "I establish my covenant with you," with the rainbow set as "the token of the covenant" (9:9, 12–17); `gods-faithfulness` — an everlasting covenant with all flesh, remembered whenever the rainbow appears (9:11–16); `sin` — even the flood's survivor falls: drunkenness, dishonor, and a curse that echoes for generations (9:20–25)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings
**Sections:**
- **9:1–17 — The Covenant of the Rainbow** (title: BSB) — God blesses Noah's sons, sets the terms for life after the flood, and seals an everlasting covenant with every living creature: the rainbow marks his promise that the waters will never again destroy the earth.
- **9:18–29 — Noah's Shame and Canaan's Curse** (title: BSB) — Noah plants a vineyard, gets drunk, and is left uncovered; Ham's disrespect and his brothers' care draw out a curse on Canaan and blessings tied to Shem and Japheth. Noah dies at nine hundred fifty.

## Genesis 10
**Summary:** The nations of the known world are traced back to Noah's three sons: Japheth's peoples spread toward the coastlands and islands; Ham's include Mizraim (Egypt), Canaan, and Nimrod, the first mighty kingdom-builder, whose realm began at Babel; and Shem's line carries the thread the story will follow. It is a genealogy with a point — every nation on earth is one spreading family, divided into lands and languages after the flood.
**Tags:** `nations-and-peoples` — the Table of Nations itself: every nation of the known world traced to one family, "after their families, according to their languages, in their lands and their nations" (10:5, 20, 31–32)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Genesis 11
**Summary:** United by one language, humanity settles in the plain of Shinar and builds a city and tower to make a name for itself rather than fill the earth. The LORD confuses their speech and scatters them, and the place is called Babel. A genealogy then descends from Shem to Abram, narrowing the story from all nations to a single family, and the chapter closes with Terah's household — including Abram, his barren wife Sarai, and Lot — leaving Ur for Canaan but stopping at Haran.
**Tags:** `humble-exaltation` — the tower-builders' bid for a great name is answered by God humbling and scattering them (11:4–9); `divine-judgment` — the LORD comes down, confuses the language of all the earth, and scatters the builders (11:5–9)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing
**Sections:**
- **11:1–9 — The Tower of Babel** (title: BSB) — The builders seek a name and security on their own terms; the LORD confuses their one language and scatters them over the face of the earth.
- **11:10–26 — Genealogy from Shem to Abram** (title: BSB) — The line of Shem is traced down through nine links to Terah and his son Abram — the family the rest of Genesis will follow.
- **11:27–32 — Terah's Descendants** (title: BSB) — Terah's family — Abram, childless Sarai, and Lot — sets out from Ur of the Chaldees for Canaan but settles in Haran, where Terah dies.

## Genesis 12
**Summary:** The LORD calls Abram to leave country, kin, and father's house for a land he will show him, promising to make of him a great nation and, through him, blessing for all the families of the earth — the turning point after Babel. Abram goes, and at Shechem and near Bethel he builds altars and calls on the LORD's name. But famine drives him to Egypt, where he passes Sarai off as his sister; the LORD strikes Pharaoh's house with plagues, and Abram is escorted out with his wife.
**Tags:** `trust-in-god` — "So Abram went, as the LORD had told him," leaving everything on the strength of a promise (12:1–4); `guidance` — called to "the land that I will show you" (12:1); `worship` — altars built at Shechem and Bethel, calling on the LORD's name (12:7–8); `honesty` — the sister-story deception and its fallout for Pharaoh's house (12:11–19); `blessing` — the fivefold blessing of the call: "I will bless you… You will be a blessing… All the families of the earth will be blessed through you" (12:2–3)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing

## Genesis 13
**Summary:** Back from Egypt and now very rich, Abram returns to his altar between Bethel and Ai. When their herdsmen quarrel because the land cannot carry both households, Abram gives Lot first choice; Lot picks the well-watered Jordan plain and moves his tent toward Sodom, whose men are exceedingly wicked. After they part, the LORD renews his promise — all the land Abram can see, and offspring like the dust of the earth — and Abram settles at Hebron and builds another altar.
**Tags:** `harmony-with-others` — "Let there be no strife between you and me... for we are relatives," and a costly concession to keep the peace (13:8–9); `gods-faithfulness` — the promise of land and offspring renewed and enlarged after the separation (13:14–17)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Genesis 14
**Summary:** When four eastern kings crush the rebellion of Sodom, Gomorrah, and their allies, they carry off Lot with the plunder. Abram arms three hundred eighteen men of his household, routs the raiders by night, and brings back Lot, the captives, and the goods. On his return, Melchizedek, priest-king of Salem, blesses Abram in the name of God Most High and receives from Abram a tenth of all — while Abram swears to keep not a thread from the king of Sodom.
**Tags:** `tithing` — the Bible's first tithe: a tenth of all, given to Melchizedek (14:20); `gods-protection` — the victory is credited to "God Most High, who has delivered your enemies into your hand" (14:20)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings
**Sections:**
- **14:1–16 — The War of the Kings** (title: BSB) — Four kings defeat five in the valley of Siddim and seize Lot; Abram pursues them past Dan and rescues his nephew with all the people and goods.
- **14:17–24 — Melchizedek Blesses Abram** (title: BSB) — Melchizedek brings out bread and wine and blesses Abram by God Most High; Abram gives the priest-king a tenth of all, but refuses any reward from Sodom's king.

## Genesis 15
**Summary:** The LORD comes to Abram in a vision — "Don't be afraid... I am your shield" — and when Abram protests that he remains childless, God points him to the stars: so will your offspring be. Abram believes the LORD, who credits it to him as righteousness. In a solemn ceremony God then binds himself to the promise, foretelling four hundred years of affliction for Abram's descendants before their return, and passing between the divided animals as smoking furnace and flaming torch to grant the land as a covenant.
**Tags:** `faith` — "He believed in the LORD, who credited it to him for righteousness" (15:6); `fear-not` — "Don't be afraid, Abram. I am your shield, your exceedingly great reward" (15:1); `gods-faithfulness` — God alone passes between the pieces, staking himself to the promise (15:17–21); `covenant` — "In that day the LORD made a covenant with Abram": the ceremony of the divided animals (15:9–18); `waiting-for-a-child` — "what will you give me, since I go childless?" (15:2–3)

## Genesis 16
**Summary:** Still childless after ten years in Canaan, Sarai gives her Egyptian servant Hagar to Abram, and the plan turns bitter: pregnant Hagar despises her mistress, Sarai treats her harshly, and Hagar flees into the wilderness. There the angel of the LORD finds her by a spring, sends her back, and promises countless offspring through her son — to be named Ishmael, "because the LORD has heard your affliction." Hagar gives God a name of her own: "You are a God who sees."
**Tags:** `pastoral-god-sees-my-suffering` — Hagar names the LORD "a God who sees" after he meets her in her misery (16:13; existing anchor); `loneliness` — a runaway servant, alone in the wilderness, is found and addressed by name (16:7–8); `waiting-for-a-child` — ten childless years in Canaan drive Sarai's plan: "the LORD has restrained me from bearing" (16:1–3); `angels` — "The LORD's angel found her by a fountain of water in the wilderness" — finding, sending back, and promising over Hagar and her son (16:7–11)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Genesis 17
**Summary:** When Abram is ninety-nine, God appears — "I am God Almighty. Walk before me and be blameless" — and confirms his covenant. Abram becomes Abraham, father of a multitude of nations; Sarai becomes Sarah, who will bear Isaac within the year; and circumcision is given as the covenant's sign for every male of the household. Abraham laughs at the thought of a hundred-year-old father and a ninety-year-old mother, and pleads for Ishmael — whom God promises to bless too — then obeys that very day.
**Tags:** `gods-faithfulness` — an everlasting covenant, restated by name to Abraham, Sarah, and the yet-unborn Isaac (17:7, 19–21); `obedience-to-the-word` — Abraham carries out the command "in the same day, as God had said to him" (17:23); `covenant` — "my covenant is with you": the everlasting covenant, named throughout the chapter and sealed in the sign of circumcision (17:2–14, 19–21)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing

## Genesis 18
**Summary:** Three visitors arrive at Abraham's tent at Mamre, and he hurries to set a meal before them. One of them — the LORD — repeats the promise with a deadline: about this time next year, Sarah will have a son. Listening at the tent door, aged Sarah laughs, and the reply hangs over the whole story: "Is anything too hard for the LORD?" As the men turn toward Sodom, the LORD confides his purpose to Abraham, who intercedes for the city, pressing from fifty righteous down to ten.
**Tags:** `prayer` — Abraham's intercession for Sodom, pressing God from fifty righteous down to ten (18:23–32); `gods-faithfulness` — the promise fixed to a set time, with nothing too hard for the LORD (18:10, 14); `hospitality` — Abraham runs from his tent door to welcome the three visitors, washing feet and setting a meal before them (18:2–8); `divine-judgment` — the outcry of Sodom brings the Judge of all the earth down to see, and Abraham appeals to his justice (18:20–21, 25)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Genesis 19
**Summary:** Two angels come to Sodom, where Lot's hospitality collides with the city's violence: the men of Sodom surround the house demanding the visitors, and Lot offers them his own daughters instead. At dawn the angels drag the lingering family out — "the LORD being merciful" — before sulfur and fire fall on Sodom and Gomorrah; Lot's wife looks back and becomes a pillar of salt. In a mountain cave afterward, Lot's daughters make their father drunk and conceive by him, bearing the ancestors of Moab and Ammon.
**Tags:** `sin` — a city whose outcry "has grown so great before the LORD" (19:13) shown in action, and its destruction (19:4–13, 24–25); `gods-protection` — Lot and his daughters are pulled bodily out of the judgment because God remembered Abraham (19:16, 29); `divine-judgment` — the LORD rains sulfur and fire on Sodom and Gomorrah, Scripture's defining picture of judgment on the cities of the plain (19:24–25, 29); `hospitality` — Lot presses the two visitors to turn aside to his house — "please come into your servant's house, stay all night, wash your feet" — and "he made them a feast, and baked unleavened bread" (19:2–3); `angels` — two angels come to Sodom, shelter under Lot's roof, and drag the lingering family to safety, "the LORD being merciful" (19:1–3, 10–22)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings
**Sections:**
- **19:1–29 — The Destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah** (title: BSB) — Lot shelters the two angels from a violent mob; they force his family out of the city, and the LORD rains sulfur and fire on the cities of the plain. Lot's wife looks back and is lost; Lot is spared for Abraham's sake.
- **19:30–38 — Lot and His Daughters** (title: BSB) — Afraid to stay in Zoar, Lot lives in a cave, where his daughters — convinced their family line is at an end — make him drunk on successive nights and each conceives by him. Their sons, Moab and Ben Ammi, father the Moabites and the Ammonites.

## Genesis 20
**Summary:** In Gerar, Abraham once more presents Sarah as his sister, and King Abimelech takes her. God warns the king in a dream — "you are a dead man" — while acknowledging that he acted in integrity, and orders Sarah restored. Abimelech confronts Abraham, who explains his fear and his half-truth, then returns Sarah with gifts and a public vindication. When Abraham prays, God heals Abimelech's household, whose wombs the LORD had closed on Sarah's account.
**Tags:** `honesty` — the half-true "she is my sister" and the damage a protective lie causes (20:2, 9–13); `pastoral-prayer-for-healing` — "Abraham prayed to God. So God healed Abimelech," his wife, and his female servants (20:17); `gods-protection` — God himself keeps Abimelech from touching Sarah, guarding her and the promise (20:3–6)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Genesis 21

# Proposed new topics

**Status (2026-08-23 confirmation pass):** all fourteen new-concept proposals below have since been admitted to the engine vocabulary with fixtures (PR #41). They are kept here for the record only; the chapter tags in this document now use them where they honestly fit. Of the four **extension candidates** at the end, two were applied as lexicon extensions with fixtures in PR #41 — `pride` folded into `humble-exaltation`, and `famine-and-scarcity` into `gods-provision` — and two were deferred (`gods-surprising-choice`, and `temptation-and-the-fall` as an extension of `remembered-a-way-of-escape`).

Merged from the two drafting workers' per-file lists (11 items from Gen 1–25, 9 items
from Gen 26–50; `blessing` and `covenant`/`covenant-promises` proposed by both).
These are Genesis themes the drafters judged to have no honest home in the current
108-concept vocabulary; nothing below is used as a tag anywhere in the chapter files.
Per the repo's data rules, none of these should be added without a golden fixture and
a gauntlet run showing measurable effect.

Two kinds of entries: **new-concept proposals**, and **extension candidates** where an
existing concept is close enough that widening its lexicon may serve better than a new id.

## New-concept proposals

1. **`covenant`** — God's covenant-making, covenant promises, and covenant signs.
   Rationale: "covenant" is the load-bearing word of Genesis 6–50 and a heavy search
   topic ("Abrahamic covenant," "God's covenant promises"); `gods-faithfulness` answers
   pastoral trust queries but is keyed to faithfulness language, not covenant queries.
   Example refs: Genesis 6:18; 9:8–17; 15:18; 17:1–14; 21:27–32; 26:3–5; 28:13–15;
   35:11–12; 46:3–4; 50:24.
   Wanted by: Gen 1–25 draft (chs. 6, 9, 15, 17, 21) and Gen 26–50 draft as
   `covenant-promises` (chs. 26, 28, 35, 46, 50) — merged here into one proposal.

2. **`blessing`** — blessing given, promised, and passed on ("bless my family,"
   "father's blessing"). Rationale: "bless" is a keyword running through the whole
   book, creation to patriarchs, and a common lay query; nothing in the vocabulary
   answers it. Example refs: Genesis 1:28; 12:2–3; 22:17; 27:27–29; 28:3–4; 32:26;
   48:15–20; 49:28.
   Wanted by: both drafts (chs. 1, 12, 22 / 27, 28, 32, 48, 49) — merged.

3. **`image-of-god`** — humanity made in God's image; human dignity. Rationale:
   `identity-in-christ` covers this only partially and awkwardly (its label and gist
   are New-Testament-specific); "image of God" and human-dignity queries deserve a
   direct concept. Review note: the Gen 1 draft's `identity-in-christ` tag was removed
   in review for this reason (see review-log.md and the open items). Example refs:
   Genesis 1:26–27; 5:1–2; 9:6.

4. **`divine-judgment`** — God's judgment on evil. Rationale: flood, Babel, and Sodom
   are among the most-searched judgment narratives; no current concept covers "God's
   judgment" queries (`hell` and `judgment-seat-of-christ` are NT-specific final-judgment
   concepts, not narrative judgment). Example refs: Genesis 6:5–7; 7:21–23; 11:8–9;
   19:24–25.

5. **`sabbath-rest`** — the hallowed seventh day. Rationale: `rest-for-the-weary` is
   shaped by Matthew 11:28 and does not honestly cover sabbath queries; Genesis 2:1–3
   is the origin text. Example refs: Genesis 2:1–3 (compare Exodus 20:8–11;
   Hebrews 4:1–11).

6. **`hospitality`** — welcoming and providing for strangers. Rationale: Genesis
   repeatedly stages hospitality as a hinge of the story, and it is a live topical
   query; `generosity` (cheerful giving) is adjacent but not the same intent.
   Example refs: Genesis 18:1–8; 19:1–3; 24:28–33; 43:16–34.

7. **`waiting-for-a-child`** — barrenness and the long wait for promised children.
   Rationale: a strong pastoral gap ("struggling to conceive," "waiting on God for a
   baby"); Genesis is the Bible's densest text on infertility. Distinct from
   `pregnancy-and-child-loss` (miscarriage/stillbirth grief) and from
   `pastoral-god-sees-my-suffering` (general seeing/hearing of affliction). Example refs:
   Genesis 11:30; 15:2–3; 16:1; 25:21; 29:31; 30:1–2, 22.

8. **`testing`** — God testing his people. Rationale: "why does God test us" queries
   have no target; Genesis 22:1 is the explicit anchor ("God tested Abraham").
   Adjacent-but-distinct: `the-lords-discipline` (correction of wrongdoing) and
   `remembered-joy-in-trials` (James 1 verse-memory). Example refs: Genesis 22:1–14.

9. **`nations-and-peoples`** — the origin and spread of the nations. Rationale:
   Genesis 10 currently supports no honest tag at all; "table of nations" / "where did
   the nations come from" queries need a home. Example refs: Genesis 10:1–32; 11:8–9;
   25:12–18; 36:1–43 (Gen 36 is also tagless today).

10. **`family-reconciliation`** — reconciliation between estranged family members.
    Rationale: `forgiving-others` and `harmony-with-others` cover parts, but neither
    answers "reconciling with my brother," "family estrangement," "healing a broken
    family" — the thing Genesis narrates more fully than any other book. Could
    alternatively be treated as a lexicon extension of `harmony-with-others`; the
    drafter's judgment was that the family-specific queries justify a distinct id.
    Example refs: Genesis 33:4; 45:1–15; 50:15–21.

11. **`providence`** — God's unseen governance of events toward his purposes ("God is
    in control," "God's plan for my life," "God meant it for good"). Rationale:
    `remembered-all-things-for-good` is a Romans 8:28 verse-memory concept and
    `trust-in-god` ("trust gods plan") covers only the trusting-response side; neither
    answers general providence queries. Example refs: Genesis 39:2–5; 45:5–8; 50:20.

12. **`dreams-and-visions`** — dreams, God speaking through dreams, interpretation of
    dreams ("dreams in the Bible," "what do dreams mean"). Rationale: load-bearing in
    at least six chapters of Genesis; `guidance` is adjacent but keyed to
    direction-for-my-life queries. Example refs: Genesis 28:12; 31:24; 37:5–10;
    40:5–19; 41:1–32; 46:2.

13. **`wrestling-with-god`** — struggling with God; refusing to let go without a
    blessing; persistence through a dark night. Distinct from `prayer` and from
    `surrender-to-god`. Example refs: Genesis 32:24–30 (echoed in 30:8).

14. **`envy-and-jealousy`** — envy, jealousy, sibling rivalry ("dealing with
    jealousy," "envious of others"). Rationale: no current concept carries these
    queries (`contentment` is the nearest neighbor but is money/circumstance-shaped).
    Example refs: Genesis 4:4–8; 26:14; 30:1; 37:11.

## Extension candidates (check before minting a new id)

These were proposed as new concepts by a drafter, but an existing concept sits close
enough that extending its lexicon may be the better move. Flagged for curation
judgment rather than resolved here.

- **`pride`** (proposed for Babel's "let's make a name for ourselves," Genesis
  11:4–9) — `humble-exaltation` already carries the humility/exaltation reversal
  ("promotion; humility; humble yourselves; god exalts the humble") and is the tag the
  Gen 11 draft uses. Plain "pride" queries could be served by extending
  `humble-exaltation`'s lexicon instead of a new id.
- **`gods-surprising-choice`** (younger over elder, the unlikely one chosen —
  Genesis 25:23; 48:14–20; 49:3–4, 8) — overlaps the same `humble-exaltation`
  reversal theme; consider folding into it (or into `pride`'s resolution above)
  before minting a third id in this space.
- **`temptation-and-the-fall`** (Genesis 3:1–7; 4:7) — plain "temptation" queries
  currently map only to `remembered-a-way-of-escape`, which is 1 Corinthians
  10:13-specific. Either mint the new concept or widen that concept's lexicon;
  note `resisting-the-devil` and `sin` already catch parts of the intent (both are
  tagged on Gen 3).
- **`famine-and-scarcity`** (Genesis 26:1; 41:53–57; 42:5; 47:13) — `gods-provision`
  covers the answer but not the situation query ("famine in the Bible," "when
  resources run out"). Extending `gods-provision`'s lexicon with famine/scarcity
  phrases may suffice.

# Decisions record — 2026-08-23 confirmation pass

The draft's five open questions — plus the front-matter policy note on untagged genealogies — were resolved in the confirmation pass as delegated defaults (items 1–6 below). The pass's remaining tag drops and replacements, and one coverage call, are recorded as items 7–9. Each call is recorded here with its rationale; Jesse can reverse any of them.

1. **Genesis 1 and "identity in Christ" — resolved.** `image-of-god` was admitted in PR #41 and Genesis 1 now carries it (1:26–27). `identity-in-christ` stays off: its label and gist are New-Testament-specific, and tagging it as *present* in Genesis 1 would read a NT doctrine back into the chapter — the same anachronism the first review removed it for. Image-of-God and human-dignity searches now land on Genesis 1 honestly.

2. **Genesis 3 and "resisting the devil" — dropped.** Genesis itself never identifies the serpent as the devil; later Scripture does (Revelation 12:9; Romans 16:20), and we affirm that identification. More decisively, the concept's substance — the believer resisting the devil, James 4:7-style — is not what Genesis 3 depicts: the chapter narrates a failure to resist temptation, so tagging it would route "resisting the devil" searches to a passage that teaches something else. Genesis 3 now carries `sin` and `divine-judgment` (3:14–19, 22–24), both honestly present. Plain "temptation" searches still have no vocabulary home; `temptation` is recorded on the tag-gap list.

3. **Untagged genealogies — both now tagged.** Genesis 10 and 36 carry `nations-and-peoples` (admitted in PR #41): the Table of Nations and the Edom record are that concept's paradigm chapters (10:5, 20, 31–32; 36:9, 31, 40–43). The honest-and-empty policy itself remains in force for future books — these two applications were resolved by a since-admitted concept, not by relaxing the bar.

4. **The proposed new topics list — resolved by PR #41.** All fourteen new-concept proposals were admitted to the vocabulary with fixtures. Of the four extension candidates, two were applied as lexicon extensions with fixtures in the same PR (`pride` → `humble-exaltation`, `famine-and-scarcity` → `gods-provision`) and two were deferred (`gods-surprising-choice`; `temptation-and-the-fall` → `remembered-a-way-of-escape`).

5. **The fall's framing — flattened to the plainer reading.** The book summary no longer states the autonomy gloss ("seized the right to judge good and evil on their own terms") as narrative fact. It now says what Genesis 3 plainly narrates: distrust of God's word and goodness, expressed in eating from the one tree God forbade (3:1, 4–6, 11, 17). The autonomy reading is a mainstream, charitable interpretation, but an interpretive frame should not stand as bare fact in the document's most-read paragraph.

6. **Grief in chapters 49–50 — dropped from 49, kept on 50.** Chapter 49's only support was the bare death verse (49:33); no mourning is described there, and a griever landing on 49 finds a blessing poem, not a grief text. All the actual mourning fills 50:1–14 — the weeping, the embalming, seventy days of Egyptian mourning, the seven-day lamentation at Atad. `pastoral-grief-and-loss` stays on chapter 50, and on 35 and 37 where loss and mourning are explicit; chapter 49 keeps its blessing, hope, and faithfulness tags.

7. **Genesis 34 — the draft's `refuge-and-justice-for-the-oppressed` dropped (vocabulary id `pastoral-refuge-and-justice`, "Justice for the oppressed").** The draft's justification was "the chapter turns on a sexual assault and the fury it provokes (34:2, 7)." Dropped because the concept is God-ward — God as the refuge and vindicator of the oppressed — and Genesis 34 never mentions God: what the chapter narrates is not refuge or divine justice but a deceitful vengeance massacre that Jacob condemns (34:30) and that 49:5–7 later curses. Routing "justice for the oppressed" searches to a chapter whose "justice" is treachery and slaughter would mislead exactly the reader that concept exists to serve. The chapter keeps `sin` and gains `honesty` (the brothers answer "with deceit," 34:13). Delegated default; Jesse can reverse by restoring the tag with the draft's justification.

8. **Genesis 45 and 50 — `remembered-all-things-for-good` replaced by `providence`.** The draft tagged both chapters with the Romans 8:28 verse-memory concept ("All things for good"). Replaced because PR #41's `providence` was minted precisely for these narrative texts — its own proposal cites 45:5–8 and 50:20 as example refs — while the `remembered-` concept is keyed to memorizing Romans 8:28 itself; carrying both would double-route the same queries and stretch a verse-memory concept over a narrative it does not quote. Delegated default; Jesse can reverse by restoring `remembered-all-things-for-good` alongside (or instead of) `providence` on either chapter.

9. **`covenant` on Genesis 9 — added; Genesis 6 considered and left without it.** Genesis 9:8–17 is the Bible's first explicit covenant enactment — "I establish my covenant with you" (9:9), the rainbow as "the token of the covenant" (9:12–17) — and now carries the tag. Genesis 6:18 is the word's first occurrence in Scripture ("I will establish my covenant with you"), but there it is a forward-looking announcement ahead of the flood, enacted only in chapter 9; Gen 6 keeps its current tags, with the promise already noted in its 6:8–22 section description. Delegated default; Jesse can add `covenant` to Genesis 6 if he wants first-mention searches to land there too.

## Edits — factual fidelity and verse ranges

1. **Gen 2 (summary):** "makes the woman from his side" → "from one of the man's
   ribs". WEB 2:21–22 says rib; "side" is an interpretive alternative not in this text.
2. **Gen 6 (sections):** ranges 6:1–8 / 6:9–22 → **6:1–7 / 6:8–22**. The BSB heading
   "Noah's Favor with God" sits at 6:8; the draft had split at the 6:9 toledot instead.
   Style rule is boundaries-from-BSB, so v8 ("Noah found favor") moves to the second
   section; both descriptions adjusted to match.
3. **Gen 17 (summary):** "parents aged one hundred and ninety" (readable as 190) →
   "a hundred-year-old father and a ninety-year-old mother" (17:17).
4. **Gen 19 (tag `sin`):** justification quoted 18:20 — an out-of-chapter ref, which
   the format forbids. Replaced with the in-chapter 19:13 ("the outcry against them
   has grown so great before the LORD").
5. **Gen 20 (tag `prayer-for-healing`):** "his servants" → "his female servants"
   (WEB 20:17).
6. **Gen 21 (sections):** ranges 21:1–7 / 21:8–21 → **21:1–8 / 21:9–21** to match the
   BSB heading position ("Sarah Turns against Hagar" at 21:9); the weaning feast (21:8)
   noted in the first section's description.
7. **Gen 25 (tag `gods-faithfulness`):** removed cross-chapter "compare 17:20";
   justification now cites only 25:11, 16.
8. **Gen 30 (tag `gods-provision`):** the quoted line "the LORD has blessed you
   wherever I turned" (30:30) is Jacob describing *Laban's* increase, but the
   justification credited it to Jacob's own increase. Replaced with Laban's admission
   "the LORD has blessed me for your sake" (30:27, 30) plus Jacob's increase (30:43).
9. **Gen 33 (summary):** altar "named 'God, the God of Israel'" → "he calls
   El Elohe Israel — 'God, the God of Israel.'" WEB 33:20 gives the name itself;
   the draft presented only a translation gloss as if it were the name.
10. **Gen 48 (summary):** "the God who has shepherded him all his life" → "the God
    who has fed him all his life long" (WEB 48:15 says "fed"; "shepherded" is an
    interpretive rendering from other translations).
11. **Section title attributions:** four titles marked "(title: adapted from BSB)"
    are in fact verbatim BSB headings — Gen 9:18–29 "Noah's Shame and Canaan's Curse",
    Gen 14:1–16 "The War of the Kings", Gen 19:1–29 "The Destruction of Sodom and
    Gomorrah", Gen 25:1–11 "The Death of Abraham". Relabeled "(title: BSB)". Convention
    applied: attribution describes the title's wording; merged ranges are visible from
    the range itself.

No other factual errors found: names, relationships, sequences, and numbers in all 50
chapter summaries and the book summary check out against the WEB text (318 men, 400
shekels, ages 127/175/137/110, ten camels, twenty pieces of silver, seventy persons,
seventy days, twelve princes, four hundred years, etc.), and all other Section ranges
match BSB heading positions and WEB chapter lengths.

## Edits — theological soundness and restraint

12. **Gen 19 (summary):** removed "to his shame" from "Lot, to his shame, offers them
    his own daughters" — a moralizing verdict the text does not state; the bare report
    is damning enough and keeps the summaries descriptive.
13. **Gen 1 (tags):** removed `identity-in-christ`. The concept's label and gist are
    New-Testament-specific ("identity in Christ"); tagging it as *present* in Genesis 1
    reads a NT doctrine back into the chapter, against the drafts' own honest-presence
    policy — and the drafters themselves proposed `image-of-god` because
    `identity-in-christ` fits "only partially and awkwardly." Gen 1 now carries one
    tag (`creation`, the existing anchor) plus the standard low-tag note. Reversible —
    see open items.

Audited with no change needed: the forward-pointing notes at 3:15, 12:1–3, 22,
49:8–12, and 50:20 all state what the text says or promises without asserting a
doctrinal verdict; the hard chapters (19, 34, 38) are handled descriptively — neither
sanitized nor sensationalized; no ranking/adjudication language and no application
moralizing found elsewhere.
