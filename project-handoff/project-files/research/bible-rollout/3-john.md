# 3 John — book summary

## Short

3 John is a personal note about hospitality in the service of the truth. The elder writes to "Gaius the beloved," commending him for faithfully receiving and sending on traveling workers who went out "for the sake of the Name" ("the Name" — the letter’s shorthand for the name of Jesus), so that those who help them become "fellow workers for the truth." Against that picture stands Diotrephes, "who loves to be first," who rejects the elder’s word and expels those who would welcome the brothers — and beside it stands Demetrius, who "has the testimony of all." The letter’s hinge is its central charge: "don’t imitate that which is evil, but that which is good."

## Full

Like 2 John, this letter comes from the writer who calls himself simply "The elder," but it is addressed to an individual: "Gaius the beloved, whom I love in truth." The opening is warm — a prayer that Gaius "may prosper in all things and be healthy, even as your soul prospers" — a personal greeting-wish that takes Gaius' thriving soul as its measure. The elder’s deepest gladness is the report brought by traveling brothers: Gaius walks in truth, and "I have no greater joy than this: to hear about my children walking in truth" (1:4).

What Gaius has been doing is the letter’s occasion. He has done "a faithful work" for brothers who were strangers to him — traveling workers of whom the letter says "for the sake of the Name they went out, taking nothing from the Gentiles." The elder asks him to keep sending such people forward "in a way worthy of God," because those who receive them become "fellow workers for the truth" (1:5–8).

Then comes the contrast: the elder had written to the assembly (the WEB’s word for the church), but Diotrephes, "who loves to be first among them," refuses to accept what the elder says — accusing him "with wicked words," refusing the brothers himself, and throwing out of the assembly those who would receive them. If the elder comes, he will call attention to those deeds. The letter distills the choice into one line to Gaius: "don’t imitate that which is evil, but that which is good. He who does good is of God" (1:11) — and offers Demetrius as the good example, a man with "the testimony of all, and of the truth itself." The elder closes as 2 John closes: many things to say, but not "with ink and pen" — he hopes to see Gaius soon and speak face to face. "Peace be to you. The friends greet you. Greet the friends by name." Small as it is, the letter leaves its readers three portraits — Gaius the faithful host, Diotrephes "who loves to be first," and Demetrius with "the testimony of all" — and a plain charge about which of them to imitate.

# The one chapter

## 3 John 1
**Summary:** The elder writes to "Gaius the beloved," praying that he "may prosper in all things and be healthy, even as your soul prospers," and rejoicing at the brothers' report that Gaius walks in truth — "I have no greater joy than this: to hear about my children walking in truth." Gaius has done "a faithful work" for brothers who were strangers — traveling workers who went out "for the sake of the Name," taking nothing from the Gentiles — and sending such workers forward on their journey "in a way worthy of God" makes their helpers "fellow workers for the truth." Diotrephes is the counter-example: he "loves to be first," does not accept the elder’s word, accuses him "with wicked words," refuses to receive the brothers himself, and forbids and throws out of the assembly those who would receive them — deeds the elder will call attention to if he comes. Gaius is charged, "don’t imitate that which is evil, but that which is good," and Demetrius is commended with "the testimony of all, and of the truth itself." The elder ends briefly — "I had many things to write to you, but I am unwilling to write to you with ink and pen" — hoping instead to speak face to face soon: "Peace be to you."

**Sections:**
- **1:1–4 — A Greeting from the Elder** (title: BSB) — The elder greets Gaius the beloved with a prayer for his health and prosperity matching his prospering soul, and rejoices at reports that Gaius walks in truth.
- **1:5–8 — Gaius Commended for Hospitality** (title: BSB) — Gaius' faithful work for traveling brothers — strangers who went out for the sake of the Name, taking nothing from the Gentiles — is to continue: sending them forward worthily of God makes their helpers fellow workers for the truth.
- **1:9–12 — Diotrephes and Demetrius** (title: BSB) — Diotrephes, who loves to be first, rejects the elder’s word, slanders him, and expels those who would receive the brothers; Gaius is told to imitate good, not evil, and Demetrius stands commended by all and by the truth itself.
- **1:13–14 — Conclusion** (title: BSB) — The elder leaves the rest unwritten, hoping to speak face to face soon, and exchanges greetings of peace between "the friends."
