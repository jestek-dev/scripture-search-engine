# Tag-gap review — consolidated for Jesse

**What this is.** A consolidation of the shared append-only tag-gap log
(`/mnt/project-files/research/bible-rollout/tag-gaps.md`, read in full as of
2026-08-23, 2,741 lines) into one deduplicated review document, organized for
decision-making. The log was written concurrently by the book-group
threads of the full-Bible chapter-summaries rollout (eight, per project
coordination records outside this directory — the log itself states no
count); this document merges each
concept's scattered rows, appends, and prose ref-merges into a single entry.

**Everything here is a PROPOSAL.** Nothing in this document is decided. Every
concept, every fold, every register split remains Jesse's call. Per the engine
covenant (repo `CLAUDE.md` and the log's own header): logging a gap does NOT
create a concept; no AI-generated data reaches the engine without a golden
fixture, a gauntlet run showing measurable effect, and a human PR merge —
`NO MEASURABLE EFFECT` means don't merge. Where this document says "the log
suggests folding X into Y," that is a recorded suggestion, not a done deal.

**What was consolidated (counts, checkable against the log).**

- Source: 2,741 lines — one shared opening table, 56 book append/delivery
  blocks, and one "Checked and already covered" section (57 `##` headings).
- 606 table data rows: **162 new-gap rows** (one per unique proposed concept id),
  **443** *(append to existing row)* rows, and **1** reference-note row
  (1 Chronicles' `kingdom-of-heaven` note, "not a firm append").
- Plus **57 prose Where-column ref-merges** recorded in block notes (Hosea 1,
  Nahum 2, Amos 2, Habakkuk 2, Ephesians 3, Haggai 2, Malachi 5, Zephaniah 4,
  2 Corinthians 2, 1 John 2, Galatians 4, Jude 2, James 6, Zechariah 11,
  Hebrews delivery 3, Revelation delivery 6), and at least two in-place merges
  attributed only retrospectively (2 Peter's and Colossians' `false-teachers`
  refs, named as "already merged" by the 1 John and Jude blocks).
- **Result: 162 unique proposed concepts**, consolidated below. A row-by-row
  trace (every raw row → its review entry) is in the working inventory the
  editor kept alongside this document.

**Book counts.** Each entry states "N books" = the number of distinct books
that logged or appended refs for that concept, as attributed by the log (row
logger + append-row loggers + recorded prose merges). A logging thread's own
cross-book citations inside its rationale (e.g. the Genesis `vengeance` row
citing Rom 12:17–21) are kept with the refs but not counted as separate
requesting books. Entries within each theme are ordered by this count,
largest first, so the ordering is checkable.

**Contents.** §1 Contested calls awaiting Jesse (5 write-ups). §2 The 162
proposed concepts in 13 themes: A Worship, temple & ritual (12); B Prophetic,

## 1. Contested calls awaiting Jesse

These are the calls the log (or a book doc it points to) explicitly leaves to

### (a) The Sadducee-resurrection tagging divergence (Matt 22 tagged; Mark 12 / Luke 20 not)

The three Synoptic parallels of the Sadducees' resurrection dispute got
divergent tag calls, deliberately left standing. `matthew.md` tags
`resurrection` on Matthew 22 ("Jesus defends the resurrection of the dead
against the Sadducees: 'God is not the God of the dead, but of the living'",
22:23, 29–32). `mark.md` declines the tag on the Mark 12:18–27 parallel
because the concept's gist is Easter-scoped ("He is risen"), and its critic
record flags the tension for Jesse — "if Jesse harmonizes, the critic suggests
doing so on the Matthew side, since Mark's call matches the concept's
Easter-scoped label" (mark.md, Decisions/round-3 notes). `luke.md` follows the
Mark precedent on Luke 20:27–40 and carries a cross-book divergence record:
"The tension is real, visible, and reversible in either direction — Jesse (or
the critic loop) can harmonize the three Synoptics to either reading"
(luke.md, "Why the divergence stands"). In the log itself, the underlying
vocabulary question is recorded as extension-check flags at the ends of the
Matthew, Mark, and Luke blocks (Acts adds a fourth witness: Acts 23:6–8;
24:15, 21), and the Isaiah-minted `resurrection-of-the-dead` row explicitly
offers those flags a home: "the curator should decide this row and those flags
together (one extension or one id, not several)."

### (b) `gentile-inclusion` vs `nations-and-peoples` (contested new-row-vs-covered)
