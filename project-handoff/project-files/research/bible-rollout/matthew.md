# Matthew — book summary (draft)

## Short

Matthew opens the New Testament by tying it to everything that came before: Jesus is "the son of David, the son of Abraham," the promised King in whom Israel's story comes to its goal. Again and again the book pauses to say that what just happened "fulfilled" what the prophets had spoken — from the virgin's child called Immanuel, "God with us," to the humble king riding into Jerusalem. Between the narratives, Matthew gathers Jesus' teaching into five great blocks, the Sermon on the Mount first among them, all announcing the Kingdom of Heaven. Rejected by his people's leaders and crucified under the placard "THIS IS JESUS, THE KING OF THE JEWS," Jesus rises and sends his disciples to all nations with the promise that closes the book: "I am with you always, even to the end of the age."

## Full

Matthew is the Gospel written to show that Jesus of Nazareth is the Messiah — the anointed King that Israel's Scriptures had promised. Its very first verse plants him in the family tree of the promises: "the book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham" (1:1). Ten times the narrator stops to point out that an event happened "that it might be fulfilled which was spoken" through the prophets — his virgin conception, the flight to Egypt, the weeping in Ramah, his home in Nazareth, his move to Galilee, his healing ministry, his gentle manner, even his habit of teaching in parables. The book opens with the child named Immanuel, "God with us" (1:23), and closes with the risen Jesus saying "I am with you always, even to the end of the age" (28:20) — the promise of God's presence brackets everything between.

The book alternates story and sermon. Five great blocks of teaching each end with the same formula, "When Jesus had finished..." (7:28; 11:1; 13:53; 19:1; 26:1): the Sermon on the Mount on the life of the Kingdom (5–7), the instructions to the twelve sent-out apostles (10), the parables of the Kingdom of Heaven (13), the teaching on life together — greatness, straying sheep, and forgiveness (18), and the words about the temple's end and the Son of Man's coming (24–25). "The Kingdom of Heaven is at hand" is the message John announces, Jesus preaches, and the Twelve carry.

The story itself moves in a long arc. A threatened infancy in Bethlehem and Egypt gives way to the preparation of John the Baptizer (our WEB translation's name for the familiar "John the Baptist"), Jesus' baptism and temptation, and a Galilean ministry of teaching, preaching, and healing that draws great multitudes — and, quickly, the opposition of the Pharisees, who by chapter 12 are conspiring to destroy him. At the hinge of the book Peter confesses, "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God" (16:16), and from that point Jesus begins telling his disciples that he must go to Jerusalem, suffer, be killed, and be raised on the third day. The final week gathers it all: the humble king's entry, the temple confrontations, the woes on the scribes and Pharisees, the Passover meal — "my blood of the new covenant, which is poured out for many for the remission of sins" (26:28) — betrayal, trial, and a cross whose accusation reads "THIS IS JESUS, THE KING OF THE JEWS" (27:37).

The ending turns the story outward. The angel's news that the crucified one is risen becomes the risen Lord's commission on a Galilean mountain: "All authority has been given to me in heaven and on earth. Go and make disciples of all nations" (28:18–19). What was glimpsed early — wise men from the east worshiping, a Roman centurion's faith, "many will come from the east and the west" (8:11) — becomes the church's marching orders, and the book that began with "God with us" leaves its readers under that same promise.

# The twenty-eight chapters

## Matthew 1
**Summary:** Matthew opens with a genealogy that runs from Abraham through King David and the exile in Babylon down to "Joseph, the husband of Mary, from whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ" — three sets of fourteen generations tying Jesus to Israel's whole story. Then comes his birth: Mary, engaged to Joseph, "was found pregnant by the Holy Spirit," and the righteous Joseph plans a quiet divorce until an angel tells him in a dream to take Mary as his wife and to name the child Jesus, "for it is he who shall save his people from their sins." The narrator adds that all this fulfills the prophet's word about the virgin's son called Immanuel — "God with us" — and Joseph wakes and does exactly as commanded.
**Tags:** `incarnation` — "that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Spirit" (1:20), and the child is named Immanuel, "which is, being interpreted, 'God with us'" (1:18–23); `salvation` — "You shall name him Jesus, for it is he who shall save his people from their sins" (1:21); `dreams-and-visions` — "an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream" to redirect Joseph's plan (1:20–24); `obedience-to-the-word` — Joseph "did as the angel of the Lord commanded him" (1:24–25); `angels` — "an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream" to turn Joseph from quiet divorce to obedient marriage and to name the child (1:20–24)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings
**Sections:**
- **1:1–17 — The Genealogy of Jesus** (title: BSB) — Three sets of fourteen generations — Abraham to David, David to the exile, the exile to the Christ — root Jesus in the covenant family, with unexpected names like Tamar, Rahab, and Ruth woven in.
- **1:18–25 — The Birth of Jesus** (title: BSB) — Mary is found pregnant by the Holy Spirit; an angel in a dream turns Joseph from quiet divorce to obedient marriage, and the child is born and named Jesus, the promised Immanuel.

## Matthew 2
**Summary:** Wise men from the east, having seen his star, come to Jerusalem asking where the King of the Jews has been born — news that troubles King Herod, whose chief priests and scribes point to Bethlehem. The star leads the visitors to the child, and they fall down, worship him, and open treasures of gold, frankincense, and myrrh, then go home another way after a dream warns them about Herod. An angel sends Joseph fleeing to Egypt with the child and his mother, and the enraged Herod kills every boy in Bethlehem two years old and under — the weeping in Ramah that Jeremiah had spoken of. After Herod dies, dreams guide the family back to the land of Israel and on to Nazareth, each move fulfilling what the prophets had spoken.
**Tags:** `worship` — the wise men come "to worship him," and at the house "they fell down and worshiped him," offering gold, frankincense, and myrrh (2:2, 11); `gods-protection` — "flee into Egypt... for Herod will seek the young child to destroy him," and the child is kept safe through every move (2:13–15, 19–22); `guidance` — "the star, which they saw in the east, went before them until it came and stood over where the young child was," and dreams direct each departure (2:9, 12–13, 19–22); `dreams-and-visions` — four dreams steer the story: the wise men warned, Joseph sent to Egypt, called back, and turned aside to Galilee (2:12, 13, 19, 22); `angels` — the angel of the Lord twice redirects the family in dreams: "flee into Egypt... for Herod will seek the young child to destroy him," then the summons home after Herod's death (2:13, 19)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Matthew 3
**Summary:** John the Baptizer appears in the wilderness of Judea preaching "Repent, for the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand!" Crowds come to be baptized in the Jordan, confessing their sins, but John blasts the Pharisees and Sadducees as an "offspring of vipers," demanding fruit worthy of repentance and warning that the ax already lies at the root of the trees. He points past himself to one mightier, who will baptize in the Holy Spirit and separate wheat from chaff. Then Jesus himself comes to be baptized — over John's protest, "to fulfill all righteousness" — and as he rises from the water the heavens open, the Spirit of God descends like a dove, and a voice declares, "This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased."
**Tags:** `repentance` — "Repent, for the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand!" and "produce fruit worthy of repentance!" (3:2, 6, 8); `baptism` — "They were baptized by him in the Jordan, confessing their sins," and Jesus is baptized to "fulfill all righteousness" (3:6, 13–16); `divine-judgment` — "the wrath to come," the ax at the root of the trees, and chaff burned "with unquenchable fire" (3:7, 10, 12); `trinity` — the chapter's climactic scene holds the three together: the Son baptized, "the Spirit of God descending as a dove," and the Father's voice, "This is my beloved Son" (3:16–17)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings
**Sections:**
- **3:1–12 — The Mission of John the Baptist** (title: BSB) — John preaches repentance in the wilderness, baptizes crowds in the Jordan, confronts the religious leaders, and announces the mightier one coming after him.
- **3:13–17 — The Baptism of Jesus** (title: BSB) — Jesus insists on baptism to fulfill all righteousness; the heavens open, the Spirit descends as a dove, and the Father's voice declares him the beloved Son.

## Matthew 4
**Summary:** Led by the Spirit into the wilderness and hungry after forty days of fasting, Jesus is tempted three times by the devil — to turn stones to bread, to leap from the temple's pinnacle, and to gain the world's kingdoms by worshiping him. Each time Jesus answers from Scripture, "It is written," and finally dismisses the tempter — "Get behind me, Satan!" — after which angels come and serve him. Hearing that John has been arrested, Jesus moves to Capernaum, fulfilling Isaiah's word about light dawning in Galilee, and begins preaching, "Repent! For the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand." Walking by the sea he calls two pairs of fishermen brothers — "Come after me, and I will make you fishers for men" — and they leave nets, boat, and father at once; his teaching and healing then draw great multitudes from the whole region.

For the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand." and preaches the Good News of the Kingdom through Galilee (4:17, 23). (The first tag was this chapter's only honest tag from the pre-2026-08-25 vocabulary; the other three were adopted as display tags 2026-08-25 — see the Decisions record.)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings
**Sections:**
- **4:1–11 — The Temptation of Jesus** (title: BSB) — Fasting in the wilderness, Jesus turns back each of the devil's three temptations with Scripture, and angels come and serve him.
- **4:12–17 — Jesus Begins His Ministry** (title: BSB) — After John's arrest Jesus settles in Capernaum — light dawning in Galilee, as Isaiah said — and takes up the message: repent, the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand.
- **4:18–22 — The First Disciples** (title: BSB) — Two pairs of fishermen brothers — Peter and Andrew, James and John — leave everything immediately at Jesus' call to become fishers for men.

## Matthew 5
**Summary:** Seeing the multitudes, Jesus goes up the mountain and opens the Sermon on the Mount with the Beatitudes — nine declarations of blessing on the poor in spirit, the mourners, the gentle, the merciful, the peacemakers, and the persecuted. He calls his hearers the salt of the earth and the light of the world, and insists he came not to destroy the law or the prophets but to fulfill them, demanding a righteousness that exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees. Six times he quotes what "was said" and answers "but I tell you," pressing the commands to the heart: anger as murder's root, lust as adultery in the heart, divorce, oaths, retaliation, and love that reaches even enemies. The chapter closes at the summit of the demand: "you shall be perfect, just as your Father in heaven is perfect."
**Tags:** `blessing` — the ninefold "Blessed are..." that opens the sermon, from the poor in spirit to the persecuted (5:3–11); `loving-others` — "love your enemies, bless those who curse you, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who mistreat you" (5:43–48); `harmony-with-others` — "Blessed are the peacemakers," and "First be reconciled to your brother, and then come and offer your gift" (5:9, 23–25); `pastoral-sexual-purity` — "everyone who gazes at a woman to lust after her has committed adultery with her already in his heart," with the radical call to cut off whatever causes stumbling (5:27–30); `pastoral-marriage-divorce-teaching` — Jesus' teaching on divorce: "whoever puts away his wife, except for the cause of sexual immorality, makes her an adulteress" (5:31–32); `honesty` — don't swear at all: "let your 'Yes' be 'Yes' and your 'No' be 'No'" (5:33–37); `oaths-and-vows` — the oath-practice teaching itself: "don't swear at all" — neither by heaven, earth, Jerusalem, nor one's own head — for whatever is more than plain speech "is of the evil one" (5:33–37); `power-of-gods-word` — Jesus' estimate of the law's permanence down to the smallest letter, inside his claim to fulfill rather than destroy: "until heaven and earth pass away, not even one smallest letter or one tiny pen stroke shall in any way pass away from the law, until all things are accomplished" (5:17–18)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Matthew 6
**Summary:** The sermon continues with a warning that runs through giving, praying, and fasting alike: don't do them "before men, to be seen by them," for the Father who sees in secret is the one who rewards. At the center Jesus gives the model prayer — "Our Father in heaven, may your name be kept holy" — asking for the Kingdom, daily bread, forgiveness, and deliverance, and adds that those who forgive others will be forgiven by the Father, while those who don't forgive won't be. He then turns to possessions: store treasures in heaven rather than earth, for the heart follows the treasure, and no one can serve two masters. The cure for anxiety is the Father's care — he feeds the birds and clothes the lilies — so "seek first God's Kingdom and his righteousness," and let tomorrow worry about itself.
**Tags:** `prayer` — teaching on how to pray, crowned by the model prayer, "Our Father in heaven, may your name be kept holy" (6:5–13); `pleasing-god-not-people` — don't give, pray, or fast "before men, to be seen by them"; "your Father who sees in secret will reward you openly" (6:1–6, 16–18); `gods-provision` — "Your heavenly Father feeds them... won't he much more clothe you," and "all these things will be given to you as well" (6:26, 30–33); `forgiving-others` — "if you forgive men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you" (6:12, 14–15); `trust-in-god` — the antidote to anxiety is confidence in the Father who "knows what things you need before you ask him" (6:8, 25–34); `heavenly-reward` — "lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven," and the Father's open reward for secret faithfulness (6:1, 4, 6, 19–21); `fasting` — direct teaching on the practice: fast washed and anointed, not "before men, to be seen by them," and "your Father who sees in secret will reward you openly" (6:16–18); `kingdom-of-heaven` — the Kingdom prayed for in the model prayer and made the disciple's first pursuit: "seek first God's Kingdom and his righteousness" (6:10, 33)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Matthew 7
**Summary:** The sermon's final stretch begins with the beam and the speck: "Don't judge, so that you won't be judged" — deal with your own eye before your brother's. Jesus invites bold prayer — "Ask, and it will be given you" — because the Father gives good things more readily than any human father, and he distills the law and the prophets into doing for others what you would want done for you. Then come the warnings in pairs: a narrow gate versus a broad way, good trees versus corrupt trees known by their fruits, and professed disciples — "Lord, Lord" — whom Jesus never knew because they didn't do the Father's will. The closing picture divides all hearers in two: the wise man who hears these words and does them builds on rock and stands through the storm; the foolish hearer who doesn't builds on sand, "and its fall was great."
**Tags:** `prayer` — "Ask, and it will be given you. Seek, and you will find. Knock, and it will be opened for you," for the Father gives good things to those who ask (7:7–11); `obedience-to-the-word` — "Not everyone who says to me, 'Lord, Lord'... but he who does the will of my Father," and the one who "hears these words of mine and does them" (7:21, 24–27); `building-on-the-rock` — the sermon's closing parable: the wise man "built his house on the rock" and it stood through rain, floods, and winds (7:24–27); `self-deception` — the beam unnoticed in one's own eye (7:3–5), and the shock of "I never knew you" falling on those who confidently say "Lord, Lord" (7:21–23); `judging-others` — the command itself: "Don't judge, so that you won't be judged," with the beam dealt with before the brother's speck (7:1–5); `false-prophets` — Jesus' fruits test in the sermon's closing warnings — appearance deceives, outcomes testify: "Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly are ravening wolves. By their fruits you will know them" (7:15–16); `jesus-the-only-way` — the sermon's closing two-ways structure: "Enter in by the narrow gate; for the gate is wide and the way is broad that leads to destruction... How narrow is the gate and the way is restricted that leads to life! There are few who find it." (7:13–14, fixture-verified; caveat carried from the concept map: in the Sermon the narrow gate is entering life on Jesus' terms — the anchor claims the two-ways structure, not a bare exclusivism slogan)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## Matthew 8
**Summary:** Down from the mountain, Jesus' authority in word is matched by authority in act: he touches and cleanses a leper, heals a centurion's paralyzed servant from a distance — marveling, "I haven't found so great a faith, not even in Israel" — cures Peter's fever-stricken mother-in-law, and with a word heals all the sick brought to him at evening, fulfilling Isaiah: "He took our infirmities and bore our diseases." To would-be followers he gives sobering terms: the Son of Man has nowhere to lay his head, and following him comes before burying one's father. Crossing the sea, he sleeps through a violent storm, then rebukes wind and waves into a great calm, leaving the men asking, "What kind of man is this?" On the far shore, two fierce demon-possessed men meet him; the demons — who call him "Son of God" — beg to enter a herd of pigs, which rushes into the sea, and the frightened city begs Jesus to leave.

## Matthew 9
**Summary:** Back in his own city, Jesus tells a paralyzed man, "your sins are forgiven you" — and when scribes silently call it blasphemy, he heals the man to show "that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins." He calls Matthew the tax collector from his tax office, eats with tax collectors and sinners, and answers the offended Pharisees, "I desire mercy, and not sacrifice," adding, "I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance"; to a question about fasting he replies that the bridegroom's friends don't mourn while he's present, and that new wine needs fresh wineskins. A ruler's plea for his dead daughter and a bleeding woman's touch of his garment both meet the same power — "Your faith has made you well," and the girl is raised — followed by two blind men healed "according to your faith" and a mute demoniac made to speak. Surveying the harassed, shepherdless crowds with compassion, Jesus tells his disciples to pray for laborers: "The harvest indeed is plentiful, but the laborers are few."

## Matthew 10
**Summary:** Jesus calls his twelve disciples, gives them authority over unclean spirits and every disease, and Matthew lists the twelve apostles by name, from Simon Peter to Judas Iscariot "who also betrayed him." He sends them — for now — not to Gentiles or Samaritans but "to the lost sheep of the house of Israel," to preach that the Kingdom of Heaven is at hand, to heal and give freely, and to travel light, letting their peace rest on worthy homes. The larger discourse looks past this journey to a costly future: sheep among wolves, floggings, betrayal within families, and hatred "for my name's sake" — yet "don't be afraid," because the Father who counts sparrows and numbers hairs values them far more, and the Spirit will give them words. Allegiance to Jesus divides even households; whoever confesses him he will confess before his Father, whoever loses life for his sake will find it, and whoever gives even a cup of cold water to a disciple "will in no way lose his reward."
**Tags:** `sharing-your-faith` — sent to "preach, saying, 'The Kingdom of Heaven is at hand!'" and to proclaim from the housetops what they heard whispered (10:5–8, 27); `suffering-for-christ` — delivered to councils, scourged, "hated by all men for my name's sake," called to take the cross and lose life for his sake (10:16–25, 38–39); `fear-not` — three times "don't be afraid": nothing hidden stays hidden, the soul is beyond men's reach, and "you are of more value than many sparrows" (10:26–31); `providence` — "Not one of them falls to the ground apart from your Father's will," and "the very hairs of your head are all numbered" (10:29–30); `heavenly-reward` — the prophet's reward, the righteous man's reward, and the giver of a cup of cold water who "will in no way lose his reward" (10:40–42)

## Matthew 11
**Summary:** From prison, John sends disciples to ask, "Are you he who comes, or should we look for another?" — and Jesus answers with the evidence of his works: the blind see, the lame walk, the dead are raised, the poor hear good news — and "Blessed is he who finds no occasion for stumbling in me." To the crowds he honors John as more than a prophet, the promised messenger and the Elijah who was to come, greatest among those born of women — yet the least in the Kingdom of Heaven is greater still; this generation, like sulking children, rejected both John's fasting and the Son of Man's feasting. Jesus then denounces Chorazin, Bethsaida, and Capernaum, the cities that saw most of his mighty works and didn't repent — it will be more tolerable for Tyre, Sidon, and Sodom on the day of judgment. But the chapter turns from woe to wonder: the Father hides these things from the wise and reveals them to infants, and Jesus issues his great invitation — "Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest."

## Matthew 23
**Summary:** Jesus tells the crowds and his disciples that the scribes and Pharisees sit on Moses' seat — observe what they tell you, but don't imitate their works, for they bind heavy burdens on others' shoulders and do all their works to be seen by men. Among his followers it must be otherwise: one teacher, one Father in heaven, and greatness through serving — for "whoever exalts himself will be humbled, and whoever humbles himself will be exalted." Then comes a cascade of woes on the scribes and Pharisees as hypocrites and blind guides — devouring widows' houses, shutting up the Kingdom, tithing herbs while leaving undone "the weightier matters of the law: justice, mercy, and faith," polishing the outside of the cup, and building prophets' tombs while filling up their fathers' measure of prophet-killing. The chapter ends not in triumph but in lament: "Jerusalem, Jerusalem... How often I would have gathered your children together, even as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, and you would not!"

## Matthew 24
**Summary:** Leaving the temple, Jesus tells his disciples that not one stone of it will be left on another, and on the Mount of Olives they ask when — and what will be the sign of his coming and of the end of the age. He describes deceivers, wars, famines, and earthquakes as "the beginning of birth pains," warns of persecution, betrayal, false prophets, and love grown cold — "but he who endures to the end will be saved" — and promises that this Good News of the Kingdom will be preached in the whole world before the end. When "the abomination of desolation" spoken of through Daniel stands in the holy place, Judea must flee, for suffering will come such as has not been from the world's beginning until now — and never will be again; then, unmistakable as lightning, the Son of Man will come on the clouds of the sky with power and great glory, and his angels will gather his chosen ones. Yet no one knows that day and hour — not the angels, but the Father only — so as in the days of Noah, when the flood came on people mid-feast, the call is to watch and be ready, like a faithful servant found at his work when his lord returns.
**Tags:** `second-coming` — the discourse's center: "they will see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of the sky with power and great glory," so "be ready, for in an hour that you don't expect, the Son of Man will come" (24:27, 30–31, 36–44); `suffering-for-christ` — "they will deliver you up to oppression and will kill you. ## Matthew 26

## Matthew 27

## Matthew 28
