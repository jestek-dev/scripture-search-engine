# Colossians — book summary (draft)

## Short

Colossians is Paul's letter, written in chains, to Christians in Colossae — a church that had never seen his face, taught by his fellow servant Epaphras. Pressure was on the church to add something to Christ: persuasive philosophy, rules about food and holy days, severe self-denial, even the worship of angels. Paul's answer is one of the New Testament's highest portraits of Jesus — the image of the invisible God, the one through whom and for whom everything was created, in whom "all the fullness of the Deity dwells bodily" — so that those who belong to him are already made full and need nothing added. The letter's second half turns that standing into a way of life: raised with Christ, believers put the old life to death, put on the new, and bring every relationship — marriage, parenting, work, speech, prayer — under the lordship of the one in whose name they are to do everything.

## Full

Paul writes with Timothy to "the saints and faithful brothers in Christ at Colossae" — believers he is bound to but has never met, part of a cluster of churches (Colossae, Laodicea, Hierapolis) served by Epaphras, who taught them the Good News (the WEB's term for the familiar "gospel"); Paul has heard of their faith in Christ Jesus, and Epaphras has declared to him their love in the Spirit. Paul is a prisoner as he writes — "for which I am also in bonds" (4:3) — and he opens the way he means to continue: with thanksgiving, and with prayer that the Colossians would be filled with "the knowledge of his will" and walk worthily of the Lord, giving thanks to the Father who "delivered us out of the power of darkness" into the Kingdom of his Son.

The letter's engine is its poem-like passage about that Son (1:15–20): he is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation, the one through whom and for whom all things — visible and invisible, thrones, dominions, principalities, powers — were created and in whom they hold together; the head of the body, the assembly (the WEB's word for the familiar "church"); the firstborn from the dead; the one in whom all the fullness was pleased to dwell and through whom God reconciles all things, "having made peace through the blood of his cross." Everything that follows leans on this. Paul calls his own imprisonment and labor a stewardship of the mystery hidden for ages and now revealed — "Christ in you, the hope of glory" — adding that he suffers gladly for the body of this Christ.

Chapter 2 turns the portrait into a defense. In the letter's own terms, the threat is persuasive speech, a "philosophy and vain deceit" running on human tradition and "the elemental spirits of the world"; judgments over eating, drinking, feast days, new moons, and Sabbaths; self-abasement and worship of the angels; and severe rules — "Don't handle, nor taste, nor touch" — that look like wisdom but "aren't of any value against the indulgence of the flesh." (The letter never names the teachers, and readers have long debated exactly who they were; what is certain is what Paul says against them.) His answer is fullness: all the fullness of the Deity dwells bodily in Christ, and in him the Colossians are made full — buried and raised with him in baptism through faith, forgiven all trespasses, the handwriting in ordinances that was against them wiped out and nailed to the cross, and every hostile power stripped and led in open triumph. People who died with Christ, Paul argues, do not owe obedience to man-made ordinances.

Chapters 3–4 draw the conclusion for daily life. Raised with Christ, believers set their minds where he reigns, because "your life is hidden with Christ in God." They put to death what belongs to the old humanity and put on the new — where "Christ is all, and in all" — dressing in compassion, forgiveness, and love, with the peace of God ruling and the word of Christ dwelling richly in thankful song. The same lordship reorders the Roman household with duties running in both directions: wives and husbands, children and fathers, household servants and masters, everyone finally answerable to the one impartial "Master in heaven." The letter closes outward and personal at once: steadfast prayer for an open door for the word, wise conduct and salted speech toward outsiders, news carried by Tychicus and Onesimus, greetings from Paul's fellow workers, letters to be exchanged with Laodicea, and a farewell in the apostle's own hand: "Remember my chains. Grace be with you."

# The four chapters

## Colossians 1
**Summary:** Paul, writing with Timothy to a church taught by Epaphras rather than by himself, opens with thanksgiving for the Colossians' faith, love, and heaven-stored hope, and with unceasing prayer that they would be filled with "the knowledge of his will" and walk worthily of the Lord, giving thanks to the Father who delivered them out of darkness into "the Kingdom of the Son of his love." The prayer rises into the letter's centerpiece: Christ is "the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation," the one through whom and for whom all things were created, in whom all things hold together, the head of the body, the assembly, in whom "all the fullness was pleased to dwell." Through him God has reconciled all things, "having made peace through the blood of his cross" — the once-alienated Colossians included, reconciled to be presented holy and blameless if they continue grounded in the faith. Paul rejoices to suffer for this body as a servant of the mystery hidden for ages and now revealed — "Christ in you, the hope of glory" — laboring to present every person perfect in Christ.
**Tags:** `deity-of-christ` — "He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation" (1:15), all things created "through him and for him" (1:16), and "all the fullness was pleased to dwell in him" (1:19); `the-cross` — God reconciles all things "having made peace through the blood of his cross" (1:20), "in the body of his flesh through death" (1:22); `forgiveness-of-sins` — "in whom we have our redemption, the forgiveness of our sins" (1:14); `prayer` — "praying always for you" (1:3); "don't cease praying and making requests for you" (1:9); `thanksgiving` — "We give thanks to God the Father" (1:3); "giving thanks to the Father" (1:12); `suffering-for-christ` — "Now I rejoice in my sufferings for your sake" (1:24); `supremacy-of-christ` — the Christ-poem's preeminence claims: "the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation," all things created "through him and for him," and "all the fullness was pleased to dwell in him" (1:15–20)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing
**Sections:**
- **1:1–2 — Greetings from Paul and Timothy** (title: BSB) — Paul, an apostle through the will of God, and Timothy greet the saints and faithful brothers at Colossae with grace and peace.
- **1:3–14 — Thanksgiving and Prayer** (title: BSB) — Constant thanks for the faith, love, and stored-up hope the Colossians learned from Epaphras, and prayer that they be filled with knowledge of God's will, walk worthily, bear fruit, and give thanks to the Father who delivered them out of darkness into his Son's Kingdom — "the forgiveness of our sins."
- **1:15–23 — The Supremacy of the Son** (title: BSB) — The Christ-poem: image of the invisible God, firstborn of all creation, creator and sustainer of all things, head of the assembly, firstborn from the dead, dwelling-place of all fullness — through whose cross God reconciles all things, the once-estranged Colossians among them.
- **1:24–29 — Paul's Suffering for the Church** (title: BSB) — Paul rejoices in his sufferings for Christ's body and explains his stewardship: the long-hidden mystery now made known — Christ in you, the hope of glory — proclaimed with all wisdom so that everyone may be presented perfect in Christ.

## Colossians 2
**Summary:** Paul tells the Colossians how greatly he struggles for them and for Laodicea — believers who have never seen his face — so that, knit together in love, they may reach full assurance in knowing the mystery of God — of the Father and of Christ — "in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden." The aim is protective: no one must delude them with persuasive speech or rob them through "philosophy and vain deceit" built on human tradition and the elemental spirits of the world rather than on Christ. Against every such offer stands the letter's key claim: "in him all the fullness of the Deity dwells bodily," and in him they are already made full — circumcised without hands, "buried with him in baptism" and raised with him through faith, forgiven all trespasses, the hostile record of ordinances nailed to the cross, the principalities and powers stripped and paraded in open triumph. Therefore no one may judge them over food, drink, feast days, new moons, or Sabbaths — "a shadow of the things to come," whose body is Christ's — and no one may disqualify them through self-abasement and worshiping of angels while failing to hold the Head. Rules like "Don't handle, nor taste, nor touch" have an appearance of wisdom in self-imposed worship and severity to the body, but no value against the flesh's indulgence; those who died with Christ are done answering to them.
**Tags:** `deity-of-christ` — "For in him all the fullness of the Deity dwells bodily" (2:9), "and in him you are made full" (2:10); `victory-in-christ` — "Having stripped the principalities and the powers, he made a show of them openly, triumphing over them in it" (2:15); `the-cross` — "the handwriting in ordinances which was against us" taken out of the way, "nailing it to the cross" (2:14); `forgiveness-of-sins` — "having forgiven us all our trespasses" (2:13); `baptism` — "having been buried with him in baptism, in which you were also raised with him through faith" (2:12); `disputable-matters` — "Let no one therefore judge you in eating or drinking, or with respect to a feast day" (2:16), such things being "a shadow of the things to come" (2:17); `false-teachers` — the chapter's protective aim: no one must delude them with persuasive speech or rob them through "philosophy and vain deceit" built on human tradition rather than on Christ (2:4, 8, 16–23); `legalism` — man-made rule-religion weighed and dismissed: "Don't handle, nor taste, nor touch" — rules with an appearance of wisdom in self-imposed worship but no value against the flesh's indulgence (2:16–23)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing

## Colossians 3
**Summary:** Because believers were "raised together with Christ," Paul tells them to seek and set their minds on the things above, where Christ sits at God's right hand: "your life is hidden with Christ in God," and when Christ, our life, is revealed, they "will also be revealed with him in glory." That new standing works out in a double movement — put to death what belongs to the earth (sexual immorality, evil desire, covetousness "which is idolatry," then anger, malice, slander, lying) and put on the new man being renewed after the image of his Creator, where the old dividing lines vanish and "Christ is all, and in all." God's chosen ones dress accordingly: compassion, kindness, humility, bearing with and "forgiving each other... even as Christ forgave you," with love binding everything together, the peace of God ruling their hearts, and the word of Christ dwelling richly in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs — whatever they do done "in the name of the Lord Jesus." The same lordship then addresses the household in turn, with a word to each member: wives and husbands, children and fathers, and household servants — who are to work heartily "as for the Lord and not for men," serving the Lord Christ, from whom comes the inheritance-reward and whose justice shows no partiality.
**Tags:** `identity-in-christ` — "For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God" (3:3); "Christ is all, and in all" (3:11); `holiness` — "Put to death therefore your members which are on the earth" (3:5), and the call to live "as God's chosen ones, holy and beloved" (3:12); `forgiving-others` — "forgiving each other... even as Christ forgave you, so you also do" (3:13); `thanksgiving` — "be thankful" (3:15); "giving thanks to God the Father through him" (3:17); `godly-marriage` — "Wives, be in subjection to your husbands" (3:18); "Husbands, love your wives, and don't be bitter against them" (3:19); `remembered-work-as-for-the-lord` — "whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord and not for men" (3:23) — the concept's own anchor text; `bondservants-and-masters` — the household code's servants' word: obedience "in singleness of heart, fearing God," working heartily for the Lord who promises "the reward of the inheritance" and repays wrong without partiality (3:11, 22–25)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing
**Sections:**
- **3:1–17 — Put On the New Self** (title: BSB) — Raised with Christ, believers aim their minds above, put the old life's practices to death, and put on the new man and its clothing — compassion, forgiveness, love, the ruling peace of God, the indwelling word, and thanksgiving in everything done in Jesus' name.
- **3:18–21 — Christian Households** (title: BSB) — Four short words inside the household, each with its own duty: wives' subjection "as is fitting in the Lord," husbands' love without bitterness, children's obedience that "pleases the Lord," and fathers' care not to provoke their children to discouragement.
- **3:22–25 — Serving with Honor** (title: BSB) — Household servants are told to obey "in singleness of heart, fearing God" — not only when watched — and to work heartily as for the Lord, who promises "the reward of the inheritance" and repays wrong without partiality; the matching duty of masters follows immediately in 4:1.

## Colossians 4
**Summary:** The household instructions finish with the masters' own duty: give servants "that which is just and equal," knowing "you also have a Master in heaven." Paul then turns the church outward — continue steadfastly in prayer with thanksgiving, praying too that God would "open to us a door for the word" even while Paul is in bonds for that word; "Walk in wisdom toward those who are outside, redeeming the time"; and keep speech always gracious, "seasoned with salt," ready to answer each one. The letter's close opens a window on Paul's circle: Tychicus and Onesimus will report everything, and greetings come from Aristarchus his fellow prisoner, Mark the cousin of Barnabas, Jesus called Justus — fellow workers who have been a comfort to him — along with Epaphras, still "striving for you in his prayers," Luke the beloved physician, and Demas. Final instructions greet Laodicea and the assembly in Nymphas' house, direct this letter and the one from Laodicea to be exchanged and read, and charge Archippus to fulfill his ministry — before Paul signs off in his own hand: "Remember my chains. Grace be with you."
**Tags:** `prayer` — "Continue steadfastly in prayer, watching in it with thanksgiving" (4:2); Epaphras "always striving for you in his prayers" (4:12); `sharing-your-faith` — praying "that God may open to us a door for the word" (4:3), and speech kept ready so "that you may know how you ought to answer each one" (4:6); `stewardship-of-days` — "Walk in wisdom toward those who are outside, redeeming the time" (4:5); `bondservants-and-masters` — the code's closing word to the other side: masters give servants "that which is just and equal," knowing "you also have a Master in heaven" (4:1); `giving-an-answer` — answering outsiders, each one particularly, with grace: speech "seasoned with salt", "that you may know how you ought to answer each one", addressed to conduct "toward those who are outside" (4:5–6; the reasoned-answer register beside `sharing-your-faith`'s open-door register)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings
**Sections:**
- **4:1–6 — Prayerful Speech and Actions** (title: BSB) — Masters owe their servants justice and equity under their own heavenly Master; the whole church owes steadfast, thankful, watchful prayer — including for an open door for Paul's message — plus wise conduct toward outsiders and grace-seasoned answers.
- **4:7–14 — Greetings from Paul's Fellow Workers** (title: BSB) — Tychicus and Onesimus will tell the Colossians everything; greetings arrive from Aristarchus, Mark, and Justus — Paul's comfort among his fellow workers of the circumcision — and from praying Epaphras, Luke the beloved physician, and Demas.
- **4:15–18 — Signature and Final Instructions** (title: BSB) — Greetings to Laodicea and Nymphas' house assembly, the instruction to exchange and read the letters, the charge to Archippus to fulfill his ministry, and Paul's handwritten farewell: "Remember my chains. Grace be with you."

## Edits — factual fidelity and WEB wording

1. **Book summary, Full ¶1:** Epaphras "brought Paul word of their faith and love" → "Paul has heard of their faith in Christ Jesus, and Epaphras has declared to him their love in the Spirit." WEB 1:8 attributes to Epaphras only "who also declared to us your love in the Spirit"; the faith-and-love report of 1:4 ("having heard of your faith… and of the love") names no reporter. The draft credited Epaphras with more than the text does.
2. **Book summary, Full ¶3:** "the record of debt nailed to the cross" → "the handwriting in ordinances that was against them wiped out and nailed to the cross." "Record of debt" is another translation's signature rendering (ESV) of 2:14; the WEB reads "wiping out the handwriting in ordinances which was against us… nailing it to the cross." §2 requires the WEB's own vocabulary.
3. **Book summary, Full ¶4:** "with Christ's peace ruling and his word dwelling richly" → "with the peace of God ruling and the word of Christ dwelling richly." WEB 3:15 reads "let the peace of God rule in your hearts" — "peace of Christ" is the ESV/NIV reading, not this text's. ("Word of Christ" is WEB 3:16, now attributed explicitly.)
4. **Colossians 3, Summary:** same error in the chapter block — "Christ's peace ruling their hearts, and his word dwelling richly in psalms, hymns, and thankful song" → "the peace of God ruling their hearts, and the word of Christ dwelling richly in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs" (WEB 3:15–16; the triad completed with the WEB's own third member instead of a truncated paraphrase).
5. **Colossians 1, Summary:** "now presented holy and blameless as they continue grounded in the faith" → "reconciled to be presented holy and blameless if they continue grounded in the faith." WEB 1:22 states the presentation as purpose ("to present you holy"), and 1:23 makes it explicitly conditional ("if it is so that you continue in the faith, grounded"); the draft stated it as an accomplished present and softened the "if."
6. **Colossians 2, Summary:** "knowing God's mystery, Christ" → "knowing the mystery of God — of the Father and of Christ." The draft's compression follows the ESV/NIV text-form ("God's mystery, which is Christ"); the WEB's 2:2 reads "the mystery of God, both of the Father and of Christ."
7. **Colossians 1, section 1:15–23 description:** "firstborn over all creation" → "firstborn of all creation." WEB 1:15 reads "of"; "over" is the NIV's rendering.
