# 2 Timothy — book summary (draft)

## Short

2 Timothy is Paul’s last letter. Writing from prison in Rome with his execution in view — "I am already being offered, and the time of my departure has come" — he calls Timothy, his "beloved child," to courage: stir up your gift, for "God didn’t give us a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and self-control"; don’t be ashamed of the Lord’s testimony or of Paul his prisoner; endure hardship for the Good News (the WEB’s term for the gospel) like a good soldier. Timothy is to pass what he has heard to faithful people who can teach others, handle "the Word of Truth" rightly against teaching that spreads like gangrene, and hold to the holy Scriptures — "God-breathed and profitable" — as grievous last-day times set in. The letter ends with Paul summing up his own life’s work — "I have fought the good fight. I have finished the course. I have kept the faith." — a crown of righteousness waiting, a plea to come before winter, and the Lord who stood by him when everyone else left.

## Full

Where 1 Timothy was working instructions for a congregation, 2 Timothy is a dying man’s personal charge. Paul writes as a prisoner expecting the end, and the letter is thick with names and memory: the tears Paul remembers, the sincere faith that "lived first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice," those in Asia who turned away, Onesiphorus who "often refreshed me, and was not ashamed of my chain." Against Timothy’s evident timidity Paul sets what God actually gave — not "a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and self-control" — and grounds courage in the Good News itself: God "saved us and called us with a holy calling, not according to our works, but according to his own purpose and grace," and Christ Jesus "abolished death, and brought life and immortality to light."

The letter’s spine is endurance and hand-off. Timothy is to suffer hardship like a soldier who stays untangled from civilian affairs, an athlete who competes by the rules, a farmer whose labor earns the first share of the crops — and to "commit the same things to faithful men who will be able to teach others also," so the message outlives both of them. The hinge is a trustworthy saying that reads like an early hymn: "For if we died with him, we will also live with him. If we endure, we will also reign with him. If we deny him, he also will deny us. If we are faithless, he remains faithful; for he can’t deny himself." (2:11–13). Meanwhile the false teachers must be met without quarreling: their word-battles consume "like gangrene" — Hymenaeus and Philetus say "the resurrection is already past" — but "God’s firm foundation stands," and the Lord’s servant corrects opponents in gentleness, hoping God "may give them repentance."

Chapter 3 widens the horizon: "in the last days, grievous times will come," and the catalog of self-love, money-love, and pleasure-love ends at religion with the shell intact — "holding a form of godliness but having denied its power." Timothy’s anchor against all of it is what he has already followed and known: Paul’s teaching and sufferings ("all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution"), and the holy Scriptures he has known from infancy, able to make him "wise for salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus" — for "Every Scripture is God-breathed and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for instruction in righteousness," equipping God’s person for every good work.

So the final charge follows, sworn before the Judge of the living and the dead: "preach the word; be urgent in season and out of season," because the time is coming when itching ears will collect teachers to suit their own lusts. Then Paul turns to his own ending — the finished course, the crown of righteousness "to all those who have loved his appearing" — and to the ache of his circumstances: Demas gone, "Only Luke is with me," bring Mark, bring the cloak and the parchments, come before winter. At his first defense everyone abandoned him, "But the Lord stood by me and strengthened me," and Paul trusts that same Lord to bring him safely to the heavenly Kingdom. The last line hands Timothy the blessing: "The Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Grace be with you. Amen."

# The four chapters

## 2 Timothy 1
**Summary:** Paul greets Timothy, "my beloved child," and the thanksgiving is unusually tender — night-and-day prayers, remembered tears, and the sincere faith that "lived first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice." On that basis comes the appeal: "stir up the gift of God which is in you," for "God didn’t give us a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and self-control." Timothy must not be ashamed of the Lord’s testimony or of Paul his prisoner, but share in hardship for the Good News — and Paul unfolds that Good News in full: saved and called by grace given "before times eternal," now revealed through the Savior "who abolished death, and brought life and immortality to light." Paul suffers for it unashamed — "I know him whom I have believed, and I am persuaded that he is able to guard that which I have committed to him against that day" — and tells Timothy to hold the pattern of sound words and guard the deposit through the Holy Spirit. The chapter closes on two contrasts: "all who are in Asia turned away from me," but Onesiphorus sought Paul out in Rome and "was not ashamed of my chain."

Yet I am not ashamed" (1:12); `grace-not-earned` — God "saved us and called us with a holy calling, not according to our works, but according to his own purpose and grace" (1:9); `assurance-of-salvation` — "I know him whom I have believed, and I am persuaded that he is able to guard that which I have committed to him against that day" (1:12); `spiritual-gifts` — "stir up the gift of God which is in you through the laying on of my hands" (1:6); `discipleship` — the mentoring bond the letter opens with: "my beloved child," the sincere faith that "lived first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice," and the pattern of sound words held "which you have heard from me" (1:2, 5, 13)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing
**Sections:**
- **1:1–2 — Paul’s Greeting to Timothy** (title: BSB) — An apostle "through the will of God, according to the promise of the life" in Christ greets his beloved child with grace, mercy, and peace.
- **1:3–12 — Faithfulness under Persecution** (title: BSB) — Thanksgiving for an inherited, sincere faith; the call to stir up the gift, refuse the spirit of fear, and suffer unashamed for a Good News that has brought life and immortality to light.
- **1:13–18 — Holding to Sound Teaching** (title: BSB) — Keep the pattern of sound words and guard the entrusted deposit by the indwelling Spirit; Asia’s desertion set against Onesiphorus’s unashamed kindness, with Paul’s prayer that he find mercy "in that day."

## 2 Timothy 2
**Summary:** "You therefore, my child, be strengthened in the grace that is in Christ Jesus" — and pass the message on: "commit the same things to faithful men who will be able to teach others also." Three pictures set the cost: the soldier who stays free of civilian entanglements to please his commander, the athlete who is not crowned "unless he has competed by the rules," the farmer who labors and so must be "the first to get a share of the crops." At the center stands the charge to "Remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead" — the Good News for which Paul is chained "as a criminal," though "God’s word isn’t chained" — and a trustworthy saying: died with him, live with him; endure, reign; deny, be denied; "If we are faithless, he remains faithful; for he can’t deny himself." Timothy must refuse word-battles and empty chatter, which eat onward "like gangrene" — Hymenaeus and Philetus have upset some people’s faith by "saying that the resurrection is already past" — yet "God’s firm foundation stands," sealed on both sides: the Lord knows his own, and those who name his name must depart from unrighteousness. So Timothy is to be a clean vessel "for honor, sanctified, and suitable for the master’s use," fleeing youthful lusts, pursuing righteousness with the pure in heart, and correcting opponents as the Lord’s gentle, patient servant — "Perhaps God may give them repentance."
**Tags:** `suffering-for-christ` — "endure hardship as a good soldier of Christ Jesus" (2:3), "I suffer hardship to the point of chains as a criminal" (2:9–10); `gods-faithfulness` — "If we are faithless, he remains faithful; for he can’t deny himself" (2:13), and "God’s firm foundation stands" (2:19); `studying-the-word` — the approved workman, "properly handling the Word of Truth" (2:15); `resurrection` — "Remember Jesus Christ, risen from the dead" (2:8), guarded against the error "that the resurrection is already past" (2:18); `holiness` — "depart from unrighteousness" (2:19), the purged "vessel for honor, sanctified" (2:21), "Flee from youthful lusts; but pursue righteousness" (2:22); `repentance` — the hope of gentle correction: "Perhaps God may give them repentance leading to a full knowledge of the truth" (2:25–26); `discipleship` — the four-generation succession charge: "commit the same things to faithful men who will be able to teach others also" (2:2); `false-teachers` — word-battles that eat onward "like gangrene": Hymenaeus and Philetus upsetting some people’s faith by "saying that the resurrection is already past" (2:16–18)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing
**Sections:**
- **2:1–13 — Grace and Perseverance** (title: BSB) — Strength in grace, the succession command of 2:2, the soldier, athlete, and farmer as pictures of undistracted endurance, the remembered risen Christ, and the trustworthy saying whose last word is that "he remains faithful; for he can’t deny himself."
- **2:14–26 — The Lord’s Approved Workman** (title: BSB) — No profitless word-fights: present yourself approved, handling the Word of Truth rightly; gangrene-teaching and its named teachers set against God’s firm foundation; vessels for honor, youthful lusts fled, and opponents corrected in gentleness toward repentance.

## 2 Timothy 3
**Summary:** "But know this: that in the last days, grievous times will come." Paul catalogs the shape of it — people who are "lovers of self, lovers of money," boastful, unthankful, "without natural affection," treacherous, "lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God" — all while "holding a form of godliness but having denied its power"; from such people Timothy must turn away. Some of them "creep into houses and take captive gullible women loaded down with sins" — captives "always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth" — and they oppose the truth "as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses," but like those magicians, their folly will become obvious. Timothy’s life runs on another track: he has followed Paul’s teaching, conduct, faith, and sufferings — at Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra — and Paul draws the sober rule, "all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution," even as he testifies, "The Lord delivered me out of them all." So the charge is to remain: in what he has learned, and in the holy Scriptures known "From infancy," which are able to make him "wise for salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus" — for "Every Scripture is God-breathed and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for instruction in righteousness," so that God’s person is "thoroughly equipped for every good work."
**Tags:** `studying-the-word` — the holy Scriptures "able to make you wise for salvation" (3:15), and "Every Scripture is God-breathed and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for instruction in righteousness" (3:16–17); `suffering-for-christ` — "all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution" (3:12), after Paul’s own persecutions "at Antioch, Iconium, and Lystra" (3:10–11); `gods-protection` — Paul’s testimony across those persecutions: "The Lord delivered me out of them all" (3:11); `end-times` — the moral-corruption catalog of the age to come: "in the last days, grievous times will come" — people "holding a form of godliness but having denied its power," "lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God" (3:1–5); `false-teachers` — the predator profile: deceivers who "creep into houses and take captive gullible women loaded down with sins," opposing the truth "as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses" — and their folly’s limit (3:6–9); `discipleship` — the followed teacher and the known source: Timothy has followed Paul’s teaching, conduct, faith, and sufferings, remaining in what he learned, "knowing from whom you have learned them" (3:10, 14); `trustworthiness-of-scripture` — inspiration and the equipping purpose as the sentence's own claims: "Every Scripture is God-breathed and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for instruction in righteousness," set against the deceivers of 3:13 (3:14–17; the verse claims sufficiency-to-equip, not canon closure — wider freight stays out of the gist; both-tags beside `studying-the-word`, which carries the engagement register of the same verses)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing
**Sections:**
- **3:1–9 — Evil in the Last Days** (title: BSB) — The last-days catalog of disordered loves, godliness kept as a form without power, predators on the gullible, and the Jannes-and-Jambres limit on how far the deception runs.
- **3:10–17 — All Scripture Is God-Breathed** (title: BSB) — Timothy’s counter-inheritance: Paul’s followed example with its persecutions and deliverances, the persecution rule for the godly, and the God-breathed Scriptures that make wise for salvation and equip for every good work.

## 2 Timothy 4
**Summary:** Paul’s last charge is sworn before God and Christ Jesus, "who will judge the living and the dead at his appearing and his Kingdom": "preach the word; be urgent in season and out of season; reprove, rebuke, and exhort with all patience and teaching" — because a time is coming when people with "itching ears" will stack up teachers to match their own lusts and "turn away to fables." Timothy must stay sober, suffer hardship, "do the work of an evangelist," and fulfill his ministry, for Paul’s own is ending — "I am already being offered, and the time of my departure has come. I have fought the good fight. I have finished the course. I have kept the faith." — and what remains is "the crown of righteousness," which the righteous judge will give "to all those who have loved his appearing." Then the letter turns achingly personal: Demas has left "having loved this present world," others are dispersed, "Only Luke is with me" — bring Mark, bring the cloak from Troas "and the books, especially the parchments"; beware Alexander the coppersmith. At Paul’s first defense "no one came to help me, but all left me" — "But the Lord stood by me and strengthened me," so that the message might be fully proclaimed and all the Gentiles hear, and Paul was "delivered out of the mouth of the lion"; the same Lord "will preserve me for his heavenly Kingdom." Greetings, one more plea to "come before winter," and the goodbye: "The Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Grace be with you. Amen."
**Tags:** `second-coming` — Christ "who will judge the living and the dead at his appearing and his Kingdom" (4:1), and the crown "to all those who have loved his appearing" (4:8); `heavenly-reward` — "the crown of righteousness is stored up for me, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will give to me on that day" (4:7–8); `sharing-your-faith` — "preach the word; be urgent in season and out of season" (4:2), "do the work of an evangelist" (4:5); `gods-faithfulness` — deserted by all, yet "the Lord stood by me and strengthened me" (4:16–17), and "the Lord will deliver me from every evil work and will preserve me for his heavenly Kingdom" (4:18); `loneliness` — "Demas left me" (4:10), "Only Luke is with me" (4:11), "no one came to help me, but all left me" (4:16); `benediction` — the letter’s closing blessing: "The Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Grace be with you. Amen." (4:22); `end-times` — the season the charge braces for: a time coming when hearers with "itching ears" will not endure sound doctrine and "turn away to fables" (4:3–4); `false-teachers` — the demand side of the same danger: teachers heaped up "after their own lusts" to match what itching ears want to hear (4:3–4)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings; bibleproject-framing
**Sections:**
- **4:1–8 — Preach the Word** (title: BSB) — The charge under oath: urgent, patient preaching against the itching-ears season to come — then Paul’s own testament: the offering poured out, the fight fought, the course finished, the crown of righteousness ahead.
- **4:9–15 — Personal Concerns** (title: BSB) — Come soon: Demas’s desertion, scattered co-workers, useful Mark, the cloak and the parchments at Troas, and a warning about Alexander the coppersmith.
- **4:16–18 — The Lord Remains Faithful** (title: BSB) — Abandoned at the first defense yet not alone: the Lord stood by Paul so that the message might be fully proclaimed to all the Gentiles, and the Lord will preserve him for the heavenly Kingdom — to him be the glory.
- **4:19–22 — Final Greetings** (title: BSB) — Greetings to Prisca and Aquila and the house of Onesiphorus, news of Erastus and sick Trophimus, come before winter, and the closing grace.
