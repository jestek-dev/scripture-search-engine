# 2 Peter — book summary

## Short

2 Peter is a reminder letter written in the shadow of the writer’s death. Opening as "Simon Peter, a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ," and presenting itself as "the second letter that I have written to you," it works to fix two things in its readers’ memory before "the putting off of my tent": that the apostolic message rests on eyewitness testimony — "we were eyewitnesses of his majesty" — and prophecy carried by the Holy Spirit, not "cunningly devised fables"; and that this trustworthy word demands growth, "adding on your part all diligence," from faith all the way up to love. Against that stand two dangers: false teachers who "will secretly bring in destructive heresies," whose "destruction will not slumber," and mockers who scoff, "Where is the promise of his coming?" The answer to both is the same: the day of the Lord will come like a thief — delayed only by a patience that wants "all" to "come to repentance" — and ends in "new heavens and a new earth, in which righteousness dwells." So: "grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ."

## Full

The letter opens with staggering generosity: God’s "divine power has granted to us all things that pertain to life and godliness," along with "precious and exceedingly great promises" through which believers "become partakers of the divine nature," having escaped the world’s corruption. But gift calls for diligence, and the letter’s first movement is a ladder of growth: "in your faith supply moral excellence; and in moral excellence, knowledge; and in knowledge, self-control; and in self-control, perseverance; and in perseverance, godliness; and in godliness, brotherly affection; and in brotherly affection, love." Abounding in these makes believers fruitful; lacking them is a kind of blindness. So the readers are to "make your calling and election sure."

Then the letter explains its own urgency. The writer knows "the putting off of my tent comes swiftly," as the Lord made clear to him, and he intends these reminders to outlive him. What he leaves is testimony: "we didn’t follow cunningly devised fables ... but we were eyewitnesses of his majesty," recalling the voice from the Majestic Glory on "the holy mountain" — "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased." Alongside the eyewitness memory stands "the more sure word of prophecy," a lamp in a dark place, for "no prophecy ever came by the will of man, but holy men of God spoke, being moved by the Holy Spirit."

Chapter 2 is a sustained denunciation, and an unsparing one: as false prophets arose in Israel, "false teachers will also be among you, who will secretly bring in destructive heresies, denying even the Master who bought them." They exploit "with deceptive words" out of covetousness; they promise "liberty, while they themselves are bondservants of corruption." The chapter’s logic is drawn from history — angels who sinned were not spared; the ancient world was flooded while Noah was preserved; Sodom and Gomorrah were turned to ashes while "righteous Lot" was delivered — so "the Lord knows how to deliver the godly out of temptation and to keep the unrighteous under punishment for the day of judgment." The portraits are grim (Balaam’s donkey rebuking a prophet’s madness, "wells without water," the dog returning to its vomit), and the letter does not soften the end of those who turn back from "the holy commandment delivered to them."

Chapter 3 answers the scoffers of the last days, who ask, "Where is the promise of his coming?" and insist that "all things continue as they were from the beginning of the creation." They "willfully forget" the flood — the world has been unmade by water once, and the present heavens and earth are "stored up for fire" against the day of judgment. Two corrections follow. First, God’s clock is not ours: "one day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day." Second, the delay is mercy: "The Lord is not slow concerning his promise, as some count slowness; but he is patient with us, not wishing that anyone should perish, but that all should come to repentance." The day will still come "as a thief in the night," dissolving the elements with fervent heat — and beyond it, "according to his promise, we look for new heavens and a new earth, in which righteousness dwells." The letter closes by commending "our beloved brother Paul," whose letters contain "some things that are hard to understand, which the ignorant and unsettled twist, as they also do to the other Scriptures," and by giving its readers their standing orders: be found in peace, beware of being carried away, and "grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. To him be the glory both now and forever."

# The three chapters

## 2 Peter 1
**Summary:** The letter opens from "Simon Peter, a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ," to those with "a like precious faith ... in the righteousness of our God and Savior, Jesus Christ." God’s divine power has already granted "all things that pertain to life and godliness" and "precious and exceedingly great promises," making believers "partakers of the divine nature" — so they must add diligence, climbing the ladder from faith through moral excellence, knowledge, self-control, perseverance, godliness, and brotherly affection to love, being "more diligent to make your calling and election sure." Knowing that "the putting off of my tent comes swiftly," the writer makes every effort so that his readers will "always be able to remember these things" even after his death. His authority is doubled: eyewitness testimony — "we were eyewitnesses of his majesty," hearing the Father’s voice on "the holy mountain" say "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased" — and "the more sure word of prophecy," a lamp in a dark place, since "no prophecy ever came by the will of man, but holy men of God spoke, being moved by the Holy Spirit."
**Tags:** `spiritual-growth` — "in your faith supply moral excellence; and in moral excellence, knowledge; and in knowledge, self-control ..." up to love, so as not to be "idle or unfruitful" (1:5–8); `assurance-of-salvation` — "be more diligent to make your calling and election sure. For if you do these things, you will never stumble" (1:10–11); `studying-the-word` — "We have the more sure word of prophecy; and you do well that you heed it as to a lamp shining in a dark place," for prophecy came as "holy men of God spoke, being moved by the Holy Spirit" (1:19–21); `second-coming` — the stated subject of the chapter’s whole closing argument is "the power and coming of our Lord Jesus Christ," certified by eyewitness testimony against "cunningly devised fables" (1:16) and by the prophetic word to be heeded "until the day dawns" (1:19) (borderline; see Decisions record item 11); `deity-of-christ` — "the righteousness of our God and Savior, Jesus Christ" (1:1), with the Father’s own voice: "This is my beloved Son" (1:17); `trustworthiness-of-scripture` — Peter’s own account of Scripture’s origin, grounding the prophetic word’s reliability: "no prophecy ever came by the will of man, but holy men of God spoke, being moved by the Holy Spirit" (1:20–21), paired with the not-myth eyewitness claim — no "cunningly devised fables," but "we were eyewitnesses of his majesty" (1:16; the context is the Transfiguration, not a resurrection-eyewitness claim; both-tags beside `studying-the-word`, which carries the heed-the-lamp register of 1:19–21)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings
**Sections:**
- **1:1–2 — A Greeting from Peter** (title: BSB) — Simon Peter greets those who share the same precious faith, wishing grace and peace multiplied in the knowledge of God and of Jesus.
- **1:3–15 — Partakers of the Divine Nature** (title: BSB) — God’s power and promises have given everything needed for life and godliness; believers respond by diligently adding virtue upon virtue, making their calling sure — reminders the writer, facing the swift "putting off of my tent," intends to outlast him.
- **1:16–21 — Eyewitnesses of His Majesty** (title: BSB) — The apostolic message is no invented fable: Peter heard the Father’s voice on the holy mountain, and the prophetic word — carried by the Holy Spirit, never by human will — shines like a lamp until the day dawns.

## 2 Peter 2
**Summary:** As false prophets once arose among the people, "false teachers will also be among you, who will secretly bring in destructive heresies, denying even the Master who bought them" — exploiting the faithful "with deceptive words" out of covetousness, with many following their immoral ways so that "the way of the truth will be maligned." Their sentence "doesn’t linger": the God who cast sinning angels down to Tartarus (the Greek name the WEB keeps for the place of imprisonment until judgment), flooded the ancient world while preserving Noah, and burned Sodom and Gomorrah as "an example to those who would live in an ungodly way" while delivering "righteous Lot," has shown both halves of the pattern — "the Lord knows how to deliver the godly out of temptation and to keep the unrighteous under punishment for the day of judgment." The teachers themselves are drawn in a run of hard images: daring despisers of authority, "wells without water," followers of Balaam "who loved the wages of wrongdoing" until his own donkey rebuked him, promising "liberty, while they themselves are bondservants of corruption." For those who escaped the world’s defilement and then turn back, "the last state has become worse ... than the first" — "The dog turns to his own vomit again."
**Tags:** `divine-judgment` — "their destruction will not slumber" (2:3): sinning angels are held in "pits of darkness to be reserved for judgment" (2:4), the flood and Sodom’s ashes stand as judgments God has already carried out (2:5–6), and "the unrighteous" are kept "under punishment for the day of judgment" (2:9); `sin` — teachers "who can’t cease from sin," with "eyes full of adultery" and hearts "trained in greed," "receiving the wages of unrighteousness" (2:13–14); `gods-protection` — God "preserved Noah" and "delivered righteous Lot," for "the Lord knows how to deliver the godly out of temptation" (2:5–9); `false-teachers` — the chapter’s subject from its first verse: "false teachers will also be among you, who will secretly bring in destructive heresies, denying even the Master who bought them," exploiting "with deceptive words" and promising "liberty, while they themselves are bondservants of corruption" (2:1–3, 17–19); `angels` — the chapter’s two angel texts: the angels who sinned, held in "pits of darkness to be reserved for judgment" (2:4), and the angels greater in might who bring no slandering judgment (2:11); `false-prophets` — the bridge verse names the OT phenomenon and the church-age phenomenon as one continuum in a single sentence: as false prophets once arose among the people, "false teachers will also be among you, who will secretly bring in destructive heresies, denying even the Master who bought them" (2:1; both-tags beside `false-teachers`, which carries the chapter’s full profile of them)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings

## 2 Peter 3
**Summary:** The writer calls this "the second letter that I have written to you," both aimed at the same thing: stirring up sincere minds to remember "the words which were spoken before by the holy prophets" and the apostles’ commandment. For "in the last days mockers will come," scoffing, "Where is the promise of his coming?" — though they "willfully forget" that the world was once unmade by water in the flood, and that the present heavens and earth are "stored up for fire" against "the day of judgment and destruction of ungodly men." Two truths steady the waiting church: "one day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day," and the delay is not slowness but patience — God "not wishing that anyone should perish, but that all should come to repentance." Even so, "the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night," dissolving the elements with fervent heat; beyond that day, "according to his promise, we look for new heavens and a new earth, in which righteousness dwells." So the fitting posture now is "holy living and godliness": regard the Lord’s patience "as salvation" — as "our beloved brother Paul" also wrote, in letters that hold "some things that are hard to understand, which the ignorant and unsettled twist" — and "grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ."
**Tags:** `second-coming` — against "Where is the promise of his coming?", "the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night," a coming to be looked for and "earnestly" desired (3:4, 10–12); `new-heaven-and-earth` — "according to his promise, we look for new heavens and a new earth, in which righteousness dwells" (3:13); `divine-judgment` — the present heavens and earth are "stored up for fire, being reserved against the day of judgment and destruction of ungodly men" (3:7, 10); `repentance` — the Lord is "patient with us, not wishing that anyone should perish, but that all should come to repentance" (3:9); `holiness` — "what kind of people ought you to be in holy living and godliness," found "in peace, without defect and blameless" (3:11, 14); `spiritual-growth` — the closing charge: "grow in the grace and knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ" (3:17–18)
**Sources:** brooks-1919; mhcc; bsb-headings
**Sections:**
- **3:1–7 — The Coming Judgment** (title: BSB) — A second reminder letter confronts last-days mockers who scoff at the promise of Christ’s coming: they willfully forget the flood, and the present heavens and earth are stored up for fire and judgment.
- **3:8–13 — The Day of the Lord** (title: BSB) — God’s timing is not ours — a day as a thousand years — and his delay is patience aimed at repentance; the day will come like a thief, dissolving the elements, and beyond it wait new heavens and a new earth where righteousness dwells.
- **3:14–18 — Final Exhortations** (title: BSB) — Waiting believers pursue peace and blamelessness, read the Lord’s patience as salvation alongside Paul’s letters — hard in places, twisted by the unsettled — and guard their steadfastness by growing in grace and knowledge of Christ.
