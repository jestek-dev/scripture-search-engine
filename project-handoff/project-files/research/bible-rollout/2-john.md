# 2 John — book summary

## Short

2 John is the shortest way the New Testament says its two inseparable things — walk in truth and love one another — and then draws the hard consequence: do not sponsor those who deny the truth. Writing "to the chosen lady and her children," the elder rejoices that some of them are "walking in truth," restates the commandment held "from the beginning" ("that we love one another"), and then warns against the "many deceivers" who deny that Jesus Christ came in the flesh: a teacher who does not bring Christ’s teaching is not to be received or welcomed, because welcoming him makes the host a participant in his work.

## Full

The letter opens "The elder, to the chosen lady and her children" — the letter’s own wording for its addressee, from a writer who identifies himself by role, not name — and the lady and her children are loved "in truth" by the elder and by "all those who know the truth." The greeting itself binds together the letter’s two key words: grace, mercy, and peace will be with us "in truth and love" (1:3). The elder’s joy is pastoral and specific — he has found some of the lady’s children "walking in truth" — and his request is nothing new, only the commandment "we had from the beginning, that we love one another," where love is defined concretely: "that we should walk according to his commandments" (1:6).

The warning that follows is the letter’s reason for existing: "many deceivers have gone out into the world" — people who do not confess "that Jesus Christ came in the flesh," and such a person is "the deceiver and the Antichrist" (1:7). The stakes are stated both for the readers ("Watch yourselves," so as not to lose what has been accomplished but to "receive a full reward") and doctrinally: whoever does not remain in the teaching of Christ "doesn’t have God," while the one who remains in the teaching "has both the Father and the Son" (1:8–9). So the instruction is blunt: a traveling teacher who does not bring this teaching is not to be received into the house or given a welcome, "for he who welcomes him participates in his evil deeds" (1:10–11). The elder ends by holding back — he has much more to say, but not "with paper and ink"; he hopes to come and speak "face to face, that our joy may be made full" — and closes with greetings from the children of the lady’s "chosen sister."

# The one chapter

## 2 John 1
**Summary:** The elder writes "to the chosen lady and her children," whom he loves "in truth," greeting them with grace, mercy, and peace "in truth and love." He rejoices greatly at having found some of her children "walking in truth," and asks — not as a new commandment, but the one held "from the beginning" — "that we love one another"; and love, he explains, means walking according to God’s commandments. Then the warning: "many deceivers have gone out into the world, those who don’t confess that Jesus Christ came in the flesh. This is the deceiver and the Antichrist." The readers must watch themselves, so that they do not lose what has been accomplished but "receive a full reward" — whoever transgresses and does not remain in Christ’s teaching "doesn’t have God," while whoever remains in the teaching has both the Father and the Son — and therefore a visitor who does not bring this teaching is not to be received into the house or welcomed, since welcoming him means participating "in his evil deeds." The elder has many things left to write, but would rather not use "paper and ink" — he hopes to come and speak "face to face, that our joy may be made full" — and he closes with greetings from the children of the lady’s "chosen sister."
**Tags:** `obedience-to-the-word` — "This is love, that we should walk according to his commandments," and children found "walking in truth, even as we have been commanded by the Father" (1:4, 6); `loving-others` — the commandment "which we had from the beginning, that we love one another" (1:5–6); `incarnation` — the dividing line of the letter: the deceivers are "those who don’t confess that Jesus Christ came in the flesh" (1:7, 9); `heavenly-reward` — "Watch yourselves, that we don’t lose the things which we have accomplished, but that we receive a full reward" (1:8); `false-teachers` — the letter’s reason for existing: "many deceivers have gone out into the world," and the teacher who does not bring Christ’s teaching is not to be received or welcomed, "for he who welcomes him participates in his evil deeds" (1:7, 10–11)

**Sections:**
- **1:1–3 — A Greeting from the Elder** (title: BSB) — The elder greets the chosen lady and her children, loved in truth by all who know the truth, with grace, mercy, and peace "in truth and love."
- **1:4–6 — Walking in the Truth** (title: BSB) — Joy at children found walking in truth leads into the old commandment restated: love one another, and love means walking according to God’s commandments.
- **1:7–11 — Beware of Deceivers** (title: BSB) — Many deceivers deny that Jesus Christ came in the flesh; remaining in Christ’s teaching is having the Father and the Son, and a teacher who does not bring it must not be received or welcomed.
- **1:12–13 — Conclusion** (title: BSB) — The elder saves the rest for a face-to-face visit, so that their joy may be made full, and passes on greetings from the children of the lady’s chosen sister.
